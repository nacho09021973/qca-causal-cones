## 0. Single question

> Given a local system with `B(c) ⊊ C_micro` but `sup|v_g| = c`, what operational
> quantity can distinguish it from a strictly microcausal theory with cone `B(c)`?

Concretely, for `ct < L < v_sub(n̂) t`:

`D = 0`, or `D > 0` and exponentially small?

## 1. Well-posedness — the decisive declaration

`‖[A(t), B]‖` and `D(L,t) = ½‖ρ_B^{(1)} − ρ_B^{(0)}‖_1` are **undefined** for the
walk of CJWW Eq. (5.15) as such: a one-particle walk on `ℓ²(Z³) ⊗ C²` carries no
algebra of local observables per region, so there is no `A_A`, no `A_B` and no
reduced state `ρ_B`. The question only becomes well posed after a lift.

**Declared lift: free-fermion (CAR) second quantization**, the standard QCA
associated with a one-particle walk and the construction under which the Pavia
Weyl QCA is the second quantization of the Weyl walk. Fix the CAR algebra over
`h = ℓ²(Z³) ⊗ C²`, the Bogoliubov automorphism `α_t(a(f)) = a(W^t f)`, and
`A_Λ := ` the algebra generated by `{a(x,i), a†(x,i) : x ∈ Λ}`. Observables are
taken **even** (parity superselection), so single `a(x)` is not admissible as a
local observable.

Alternatives **not** taken, and why: a bosonic lift changes the local algebra
without changing the single-particle kernel that controls the answer; a
"single-particle surrogate" defining `D` from the position distribution alone
would collapse this gate back into Sec. 8.8 and answer nothing new.

**Every conclusion of this gate is a statement about the free-fermion lift of
(5.15), not about the one-particle walk**, and must be written that way.

## 2. Frozen target and admitted method

Target: the free-fermion lift of CJWW Eq. (5.15) only, in the normalization of

## 3. Declared hypotheses, stated before solving

- **H1 (kernel criterion).** For even local `A` at `x` and `B` at `y`,
  `[α_t(A), B] ≠ 0` iff the single-particle kernel `(W^t)_{y,x} ≠ 0`; so the
  causal structure of the lift is exactly the strict support of `W^t`.
- **H2 (answer).** For `ct < L < v_sub(n̂) t` the commutator is **nonzero** and
  exponentially small in `t`; at the vertex rays its size is set by
  `||(W_σ)^t|| = 4^{-t}` (Sec. 8.5).
- **H3 (angular signature).** Along the six axial directions, where
  `v_sub(ê_i) = c`, the commutator is **exactly zero** for every `L > ct`: no
  superluminal tail exists there at all. The effect is strictly off-axis.
- **H4 (outer boundary).** For `L > v_sub(n̂) t` the commutator is exactly zero,
  so `C_micro` is the exact operational causal boundary of the lift.

Refutation of any `Hi` is a valid outcome. If instead all observable signalling
turns out to vanish despite the wider support, that is an equally decisive
outcome and must be recorded as such: it would mean a mathematically wider
substrate is **not** physically accessible by this mechanism.

## 4. Explicit exclusions

- **No claim about our universe.** Nothing here is evidence that
  `c_universe < ` the fundamental causal limit (`CURRENT_STATE.md` Sec. 8.9(iii),
  status UNKNOWN). Asserting it would be the category error already flagged there.
- **No experimental/constraint survey.** LIV dispersion bounds, threshold
  processes, sidereal-modulation searches and the SME tables are **out of scope**
  for this gate. Per the authorizing instruction, data come only after the
  theoretical question resolves, and only under a further charter.
- **No cubic-lattice assumption for physical space.** `v_sub(n̂) = ||n̂||_1` is a
  property of this model, not a prediction about nature.
- **No interacting QCA, no new substrate class**, per the Sec. 8.7 scope decision.
- **Falsifiability asymmetry, recorded.** That `c` is *not* the fundamental
  causal limit is falsifiable in principle; that it *is* exactly the limit is
  not establishable by any finite set of null results, since a wider cone fully
  decoupled from accessible observables would be invisible. No conclusion of
  this gate may be phrased as confirming exact coincidence.
