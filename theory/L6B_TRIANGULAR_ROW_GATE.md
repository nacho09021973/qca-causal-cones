## 1. Why L6b was left OPEN, and what this charter changes

Recorded reason (parent verifier, `SOURCE_GAPS["L6b"]`): the edge geometry
and the routing `e(v,k)` are fixed only by Fig. 1, not by the text.

Reading of the source on 2026-11-02 (p.4, text and Fig. 1), to be
re-checked at execution:

- State space: one `C^2` per edge shared by two triangles; upper component
  on the white triangle's side, lower on the grey's; "each triangle hosts
  a `C^3` vector".
- Dynamics: `R` rotates every triangle anticlockwise (side `k -> k+1 mod
  3`), then the fixed `W` of Eq. (9) acts on every edge (Eqs. (11)–(12)).
- White and grey triangles "have the same orientation" (p.4).

Consequence for the parent declaration: the translations that preserve the
colouring are those of the white-triangle lattice; a unit cell holds three
edges. As a translation-invariant walk on that Bravais lattice the internal
dimension is therefore `s = 6`, not the `s = 2` declared in the parent
table. This is a reading, `[UNVERIFIED]` until the symbol is built; the
execution computes `s` and records any disagreement with the parent table
instead of repairing it.

## 2. Encoding rule (removes the interpretive choice)

The text constrains the labelling but Fig. 1 is what fixes it. Instead of
choosing one reading of the figure:

1. Enumerate every assignment of side labels `k = 0,1,2` to white and grey
   triangles, and every neighbour map `e(v,k)`, that satisfies the textual
   constraints of §1 (equilateral triangles; anticlockwise `R`; same
   orientation of white and grey; upper component on white sides;
   translation invariance on the white-triangle lattice).

3. Record which assignment matches Fig. 1 as read, as a cross-check only.
