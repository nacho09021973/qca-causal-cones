## 1. Domain and reduction

The lattice is Lambda/3 after physical maximum-radius-one normalization,
g1=(1,0), g2=(1/2,sqrt(3)/2). In p=k.g1/3, q=k.g2/3 the frequency set is

    {(i,j) in Z^2: max(|i|,|j|,|i+j|)<=3}.

The permanent verifier counts 37 points. The exact hull is the regular
hexagon with vertices R^j*g1 and inradius sqrt(3)/2. This is the aligned
radius-three lattice ball, not every possible hexagon or orientation.

The reviewed reduction (reports/2026-11-05--hex-trace-scalar-method.md
Sec. 3; Claude Opus 5.5, PASS with no mandatory repairs) gives, after
D6 averaging, every possible scalar candidate as

    f = f0 + Delta*(alpha+beta*S1), alpha,beta real,
    f0 = [cos(3p)+cos(3q)+cos(3(p-q))-1]/2,
    Delta = sin^2(p/2)*sin^2(q/2)*sin^2((p-q)/2),
    S1 = 2[cos p+cos q+cos(p-q)].

Indeed the residual support has radius one, whose only D6 orbits are the
origin and the six-point ring. Averaging preserves bounds, support, f(0)=1
and normal cosines, so excluding symmetric candidates excludes arbitrary
real candidates with these scalar conditions on this aligned support.
We seek -1<=f<=1 globally; point samples alone cannot prove feasibility,
but an incompatible finite subset proves impossibility.

## 2. Four exact point restrictions suffice

Use only p=2q, x=cos(q) in [-1,1]. Then

    f0(x) = [T6(x)+2*T3(x)-1]/2,
    Delta(x) = (1-x^2)*(1-x)^2/4,
    S1(x) = 4*x^2+4*x-2,

where Tj is the Chebyshev polynomial defined by cos(jq)=Tj(cos q).
These are ordinary identities, not a boundedness approximation.

For Delta>0, the upper and lower global bounds require respectively

    alpha+S1*beta <= (1-f0)/Delta,
    -alpha-S1*beta <= (1+f0)/Delta.

Four realizable points give these exact inequalities:

| x=cos(q), p=2q | Bound used | Necessary inequality |
|---|---|---|
| 5/16 | upper | alpha-(23/64)*beta <= 204997/10164 |
| -1/2 | lower | -alpha+3*beta <= 128/27 |
| 3/8 | upper | alpha+(1/16)*beta <= 6811/275 |
| 13/16 | lower | -alpha-(249/64)*beta <= -7475/108 |

Multiply the first two inequalities by 64/169 each, and the last two by
64/245 each. All four multipliers are strictly positive. In the sum,
the alpha terms cancel; the first pair contributes +beta and the second
pair contributes -beta. The right side is exactly

    (64/169)*(204997/10164+128/27)
    + (64/245)*(6811/275-7475/108)
    = -7362262528/3381753375 < 0.

Thus the global bounds imply 0<=-7362262528/3381753375, a contradiction.
No real alpha,beta can satisfy even these four restrictions.
