# What causal geometry determines about the Weyl speed on N3

Documentary synthesis of the results recorded at `cff4bd9`. The question is whether those results determine the speed from causal geometry. The stopping criterion is to retain a bound or a family-specific statement at its proved scope if the evidence yields no selection law.

**Proposition.** Fix the normalized BCC neighbourhood in three dimensions with jumps $(\pm1,\pm1,\pm1)/\sqrt3$ and one complete update per unit time. Take any translation-invariant unitary symbol with exactly these eight active modes, a fixed finite internal dimension $s\ge2$, and covariance under a fixed unitary or projective representation of the Cartesian half-turn group $D_2$. At the origin select a full eigenphase cluster of rank at least two, separated from any other eigenphases there. Assume that in every unit direction the cluster contains the slopes $+c_0,-c_0$ of an untilted isotropic cone, with $c_0>0$. Then

$$
0<c_0\le c_{\rm strict}^{\rm iso}\le r_{\rm in}(C_{\rm micro})=1/\sqrt3 .
$$

Even fixing the entire strict causal body, the internal dimension and the symmetry representation can leave a continuum of possible speeds across rules. In the reviewed four-component family at fixed Floquet angle $\pi/4$, the body is the same cube for every mixing parameter $0<a<1$, and $c_0/c_{\rm strict}^{\rm iso}=a$.

**Proof from the existing results.** Let $S_n$ be the exact support of the full symbol after $n$ updates and $H_n(q)=\max_{x\in S_n}q\cdot x$. Compression of the directional derivative to the original eigenphase cluster gives $n c_0\le H_n(q)$ for every unit $q$. Subadditivity gives the directional limit of $H_n(q)/n$. Its minimum over directions is $c_{\rm strict}^{\rm iso}$. Since $S_n/n\subseteq C_{\rm micro}$, this minimum is at most the cube's inradius. In the four-component construction each extreme vertex survives every power, whereas the projected generators have Weyl slope $a/\sqrt3$. The two facts together establish the freedom at fixed geometry. ## Directional geometry and the doubled-slope example

Known evidence is the reviewed family at $\theta=\pi/4$ and its exact support. The documentary question is what a comparison called “twice $c$” means. Any interpretation requiring a propagation front or a physical identification exceeds this evidence and ends that interpretation here.

In this family the microscopic and strict causal bodies both equal the cube $[-1/\sqrt3,\,1/\sqrt3]^3$. Their inradius is $1/\sqrt3$, and their circumradius is $1$. For a unit normal $u$ the support function is $h(u)=(|u_x|+|u_y|+|u_z|)/\sqrt3$. It takes the value $1/\sqrt3$ on coordinate axes and $1$ on body diagonals. These are direct geometric deductions from the cube. For a general direction, the support function bounds the projection onto its normal. The radial boundary for a velocity vector parallel to $u$ is instead $\rho(u)=1/(\sqrt3\,\max_i|u_i|)$. The two functions agree exactly when all nonzero components of $u$ have equal magnitude: on the coordinate axes, the face diagonals and the body diagonals.

With the same space and time calibration, compare $a=1/3$ and $a=2/3$ at $\theta=\pi/4$. The second rule has twice the first rule's Weyl slope, and both slopes lie below the common inradius.
