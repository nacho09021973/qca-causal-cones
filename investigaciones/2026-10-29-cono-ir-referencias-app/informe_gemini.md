#### 1. Chadha and Nielsen (1983)
* **What the source establishes:** Published in *Nuclear Physics B* in **1983** (from a 1982 Niels Bohr Institute preprint `NBI-HE-82-42`). The authors analyze 3+1D Quantum Electrodynamics (QED)—both for a single charged fermion coupled to an electromagnetic field and for multiple fermion species with distinct limiting velocities—broken explicitly by non-covariant kinetic tensors ($\eta^{\mu\nu}$). Using a **one-loop renormalization group (RG) approximation** in the weak gauge coupling $e$ (specifically $\alpha = e^2/4\pi$) and expanding to first order in the Lorentz-violating parameters, they show that the velocity differences (and spatial anisotropies) run toward zero in the infrared (IR) as a power law of the energy scale $\mu$: $\Delta c(\mu) \propto (\mu/\Lambda)^{\beta}$ with a positive anomalous dimension $\beta = \frac{8\alpha}{3\pi} > 0$.
* **Fidelity of the app's claim:**
  * **Year:** **Incorrect.** The paper is from **1983** (preprint 1982), not 1976. *(Inference: The app likely confused the date with Nielsen and Ninomiya's 1978 work $\beta$-functions in non-covariant Yang-Mills, Nucl. Phys. B 141, 153).*
  * **Physics claim:** **Faithful regarding the IR attractor, with an important caveat on the speed of running.** The paper indeed establishes that gauge interactions drive velocity differences between species to zero in the IR. However, because the running is logarithmic (or a very slow power law $\sim (\mu/\Lambda)^{\mathcal{O}(\alpha)}$), the authors themselves note (and later literature emphasizes) that starting from $\mathcal{O}(1)$ Lorentz violation at the Planck scale, this one-loop QED flow is far too slow to reach experimental bounds ($\Delta c \lesssim 10^{-21}$) at accessible energies without additional mechanisms or strong coupling.

#### 2. Volovik on Fermi Points and Emergent Lorentz Invariance
* **What the source establishes:** In *The Universe in a Helium Droplet* (2003, especially Chapters 7–9; also available open-access on Volovik's institutional homepage and summarized in *Phys. Rept.* **351**, 195, 2001), Volovik formulates momentum-space topology for gapless fermionic systems. A Fermi point (Weyl node) in 3D momentum space is a point singularity of the two-point Green's function $G(\omega, \mathbf{p})$ protected by the topological winding number:
  $$N_3 = \frac{1}{24\pi^2} \epsilon_{\mu\nu\lambda\gamma}\,\text{tr} \oint_{\Sigma_3} dS^\gamma\, G\partial_\mu G^{-1} G\partial_\nu G^{-1} G\partial_\lambda G^{-1} \in \mathbb{Z}$$
  Because $N_3$ is a topological invariant, small perturbations cannot open a gap (unless two nodes of opposite charge annihilate). Expanding $G^{-1}(p_\mu)$ linearly around the node $p_\mu^{(0)}$ automatically yields a relativistic Weyl spectrum $g^{\mu\nu}(p_\mu - p_\mu^{(0)})(p_\nu - p_\nu^{(0)}) = 0$, where the linear expansion coefficients form an emergent vierbein $e^\mu_a$ (and effective metric $g^{\mu\nu}$) and the shift of the node acts as an emergent gauge field $A_\mu$.
* **Fidelity of the app's claim:** **Partially faithful, but overstates "absolute rigidity" and cross-species universality.**
  * *What topology protects:* $N_3$ strictly protects the **existence of the gapless node** and the **linear (conical) dispersion** $\omega \sim v_{ij}\delta p^j$ in the limit $p \to p^{(0)}$. For a *single* irreducible Fermi point (or a degenerate one protected by an internal symmetry), the effective metric $g^{\mu\nu}$ defines an exact emergent Lorentz symmetry in the asymptotic IR limit ($E \to 0$).
  * *What topology does NOT protect:* First, topology alone does **not** force different fermion species (or separated Fermi points without a connecting discrete/internal symmetry) to share the **same** emergent vierbein or a common limiting speed $c$. As Volovik explicitly discusses, in general condensed-matter realizations (such as $^3\text{He-A}$), different quasiparticle branches or collective bosonic modes (like orbital waves vs. sound waves) have different propagation speeds; sharing a single common metric requires either an exact underlying symmetry linking the species or an additional RG mechanism (like Chadha–Nielsen). Second, Lorentz invariance is **not** "absolutely rigid" at finite low energies: higher-order non-relativistic gradient terms in the expansion around the Fermi point break Lorentz symmetry at any non-zero energy, and in the deep IR, non-analytic one-loop corrections from interactions among modes with different speeds can cause a "reentrant violation of special relativity" (which Volovik explicitly analyzed in *JETP Lett.* **73**, 161, 2001, `hep-ph/0101286`).

#### 3. Collins, Perez, Sudarsky, Urrutia, and Vucetich (2004)
* **What the source establishes:** In *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **93**, 191301 (2004) (`gr-qc/0403053`), the authors study a Yukawa theory (a fermion coupled to a scalar with coupling $g$) in which Lorentz invariance is broken only at the UV cutoff scale $\Lambda$ (taken to be $\sim M_{\text{Pl}}$) via a modified propagator cutoff function $f(|\mathbf{k}|/\Lambda)$ in a preferred frame. Computing the one-loop self-energy $\Sigma(p)$, they expand for small external four-momentum:
  $$\Sigma(p) = A + p^2 B + m\,\not\!p\, C + (p\cdot W)^2 \tilde{B} + (p\cdot W)(\not\!W)\,m\,\tilde{C} + \mathcal{O}(p^4/\Lambda^2)$$
  where $W^\mu = (1,\mathbf{0})$ is the preferred-frame four-vector. While tree-level dispersion modifications are suppressed by $(E/\Lambda)^2$, the radiative correction to the dimension-4 kinetic term (the velocity shift $\xi$ or $\tilde{B}, \tilde{C}$) is **unsuppressed** by powers of $\Lambda$:
  $$\xi = \frac{g^2}{4\pi^2} \left[ d_1 + d_2 \ln\left(\frac{\Lambda^2}{m^2}\right) \right] \sim \mathcal{O}\left(\frac{g^2}{4\pi^2}\right)$$
  That is, the quadratically divergent loop integrals $\int^\Lambda d^4k \, k^\mu k^\nu / k^6$ cancel the $1/\Lambda^2$ suppression of the UV cutoff modification, leaving an $\mathcal{O}(g^2)$ ("percent level") Lorentz violation at low energies. Because clock-comparison and astrophysical experiments bound dimension-4 velocity differences to $|\delta c / c| \lesssim 10^{-20}\text{--}10^{-22}$ (the abstract states *"at the percent level, some 20 orders of magnitude higher than earlier estimates"*), bare parameters must be fine-tuned to roughly 1 part in $10^{20}$.

#### 8. IceCube PeV Neutrinos and Cohen–Glashow Constraints
* **What the sources establish:**

Lett.* 107, 181803 (`arXiv:1109.6562`):** Originally written in response to the OPERA anomaly, this paper shows that if neutrinos are superluminal with $v_\nu^2 - c^2 = \delta_\nu > 0$ (assuming standard electron dispersion, or defining $\delta_{\nu e} = \delta_\nu - \delta_e > 0$), three vacuum decay channels become kinematically allowed above threshold: vacuum Cherenkov ($\nu \to \nu \gamma$), neutrino splitting ($\nu \to \nu \nu \bar{\nu}$), and—most stringently—**vacuum electron-positron pair emission (VPE)**, $\nu \to \nu e^+ e^-$. #### 9. Vacuum Cherenkov Bound on Electron Lorentz Violation from ~100 TeV Electrons (Crab Nebula)
* **What the sources establish:**
  * **Primary foundational paper on Crab Nebula QED bounds:** **T. Jacobson, S. Liberati, and D. Mattingly (2003), *Nature* 424, 1019–1021 (`astro-ph/0212190`)** (expanded in *Phys. Rev. D* **67**, 124011, 2003, `hep-ph/0209264`, and *Annals Phys.* **321**, 150–196, 2006, `astro-ph/0505267`).
  * **What it establishes (and the distinction between Vacuum Cherenkov and Synchrotron):** For a modified dispersion relation $E^2 = p^2 + m_e^2 + \eta (p^3 / M_{\text{Pl}})$ (or dimension-4 velocity shift $\delta_e = c_e - c_\gamma$):
    1. **Superluminal electrons ($\eta_e > 0$ or $\delta_e > 0$): Vacuum Cherenkov ($e^- \to e^- \gamma$).** Because inverse-Compton (IC) gamma rays from the Crab Nebula were observed up to $E_\gamma \approx 50\text{ TeV}$ (by HEGRA in 2003, and later $>100\text{ TeV}$ by HAWC/Tibet AS$\gamma$ in 2019 and $\sim 1\text{ PeV}$ by LHAASO in 2021), uninhibited electrons of energy $E_e \gtrsim 50\text{--}100\text{ TeV}$ must exist in the nebula without losing energy via instantaneous vacuum Cherenkov radiation.
