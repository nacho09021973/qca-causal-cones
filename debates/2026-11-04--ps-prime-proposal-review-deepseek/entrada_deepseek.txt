## 2. Two quantifiers requiring separate decisions

Write `P` for a presentation, `r(P)` for its number of reduced directions,
and `W` for the complete walk.

1. **Presentations of one walk.** “For every presentation `P` of `W`”,
   “there exists a presentation `P` of `W`”, and “for a selected
   presentation `P_0(W)`” are different conditions. The example separates
   the count of a presentation from the complete operator. Choosing one
   of these quantifiers would add a definition; none is adopted here.
2. **Walks on one substrate.** Existence of a saturating walk in a
   substrate's declared class is different from saying that each walk
   with a particular factorization saturates. Contract Sec. 2.1 uses the
   first reading for saturation. A future formulation must say whether
   the generator test applies to the saturating witness, to every walk
   on the substrate, or to the existence of some walk. It must not
   silently replace the substrate-level statement with a per-walk one.

## 3. Options and their outstanding obligations

| Option | What it defines | Outstanding obligation / reason not to adopt it here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Printed presentation | A property of the published construction | Traceable but presentation-dependent, not intrinsic to `W` |
| Combine compatible factors until no further combination is possible | A reduction of a presentation | Complete rules and invariance under different reduction sequences are missing; L6e supplies only one concrete reduction |
| Some presentation has `d` independent reduced directions | A logical property of `W`, once the permitted presentations are fixed | The permitted set must be defined; an existence witness does not decide nonexistence in other rows |
| Minimum direction count over permitted presentations | A candidate intrinsic property, if the domain is defined and nonempty | No minimum has been established for the rows; this is a new definition, not an editorial repair |
| Admit only presentations whose zonotope equals `conv F` | An explicit geometric restriction | The vertex condition does not show that such a presentation exists for each walk; membership and factorization remain unresolved |

A rule cannot be justified by making the saturating rows have `d`
directions and the other rows not. Its selection must be stated and
assessed without consulting `c_0 = r_in`.

The last option uses the corrected F2 but has a precise limit. Conditional
on `Z(P) = conv F`, and on the generators spanning `R^d` (a
full-dimensional body, which holds in every row of the record;
clarification A2), the geometric statement in contract Sec. 1 makes
“exactly `d` independent reduced directions” equivalent to the common body
being a parallelepiped. The truth value of that predicate is then fixed
by the body, even without a canonical factorization. This is a conditional
consequence of the stated geometric criterion, not proof that suitable
presentations exist or a theorem about saturation. Under this restriction,
a PS′ reformulation would use a body predicate involving parallelepipeds;
PH uses hypercubes. Those predicates remain distinct. The walk/substrate
quantifiers in Sec. 2 would still need to be fixed before stating a new
biconditional.

The domain also matters for G1-a. Centering asymmetric shifts may introduce
half-vectors that are not translations of the original group at an
intermediate step. Zero total center displacement over the complete update
does not by itself admit those factors as operators on the original
space. A rule must specify the permitted group and space for intermediate
steps. No such extension is adopted here.

## 1. Statement under audit

> **(PH) [UNVERIFIED], provisional predicate.** For `s = 2`, `d <= 3`,
> within the declared classes of Sec. 2 and only for models with an

Two directions are kept apart:

- **(PH⇐)** `C_micro` a hypercube up to scale and isometry ⇒ there exists

  admissible walk on that substrate has `c_0 < r_in`.

A body with no admissible isotropic untilted node in the declared class is
outside the statement: it is on neither side of the biconditional and is
recorded as `OUTSIDE_DEFINITIONS` (Sec. 3.4).

**The predicate is a geometric classification suggested by the existing

This contract states only (PH) on the promoted rows. No general statement

**Zonotope reading (PS′) [UNVERIFIED], product class only, not equivalent
to (PH).** For a walk that is a product of `n` coin–partial-shift factors,
each partial shift moving by `±a_i`, the composite step set is
`F = {sum_i ε_i a_i}` and `C_micro = conv F` is the zonotope
`sum_i [-a_i, a_i]` (convex hull of a Minkowski sum is the Minkowski sum
of the hulls). It is a parallelepiped exactly when the reduced generators
(`a_i` up to sign and positive scale, parallel ones merged) are `d`
linearly independent vectors. (PS′) reads: in the product class, the
ceiling is attained iff the generators reduce to `d` independent
directions. **(PS′) is not equivalent to (PH):** `d` independent
generators need not be orthogonal or of equal length, so a parallelepiped
need not be a hypercube (e.g. a 60° rhombus in `d = 2`). No equivalence is
claimed until proved. Examples in the promoted rows: square (two
generators), BCC/cube (three orthogonal generators), `W_hex` (three
generators at 120°, `d = 2`: the hexagon). The zonotope facts it would use

## 2. Quantifiers, domain and normalization

1. **Quantifiers.** "The ceiling is attained on a substrate" means: there
   exists at least one admissible walk `W` on that substrate with

   non-saturating walk on a substrate where another walk saturates is not
   a counterexample to (PH⇐) (e.g. L3s4 on BCC, which in any case has
   `s = 4` and is outside the domain).
2. **Domain.** `s = 2`, `d <= 3`, models with an admissible isotropic
   untilted node, and only the classes already declared in the frozen
   contracts the promoted rows cite (C_ACROSS_LATTICES_GATE,
   HEX_CEILING_GATE, HEX_SUPREMUM_GATE and the row contracts).
   **"Admissible"** means exactly: the walk belongs to one of these declared
   classes and has an isotropic untilted node at `k = 0` in the sense of
   Sec. 2.4 (`U(0)` scalar, traceless Hermitian linear term with
   `{T_a, T_b} = 2 c_0^2 delta_ab`, zero tilt; equivalently
   `A(n)^2 = c_0^2 |n|^2 I`, HEX_CEILING_GATE Sec. 1). It is not the
   "admissible graph" of P17, whose isotropy in its own sense excludes the
   triangular lattice. Rows with
   `s != 2` are recorded and are outside the domain: L5b and L3s4
   (`s = 4`, c-across-lattices report Sec. 4 table); L6b (`s = 6`: L6b
   report Secs. 1–2, `s = 6` counted per cell, three edges with `C^2`
   each, whereas the parent's `s = 2` counts per edge; the row is promoted

   and still list L6b with `s = 2` (`OPEN`, not encoded); the exclusion
   rests on the promoted `s = 6`. No extrapolation to other `s`, `d`,
   lattices or classes.
3. **Substrate.** As in C_ACROSS_LATTICES_GATE Sec. 2: the translation group
   generated by one complete update together with its composite step set.
   Here `N := F`, the composite step set of one complete update (all sums
   of one displacement per partial shift), not the displacement set of a
   single partial shift. `0 ∈ N` does not change `C_micro`, `r_in` or the
   longest step when it is interior.
4. **Normalization and single source per row.** Every row is compared with
   the same frozen definitions (CS Sec. 2): `C_micro := conv(N)`; `h_C`;

   the massless node (`{T_a, T_b} = 2 c_0^2 delta_ab`, as certified per row

   update per time unit. **For each row the pair `(r_in, c_0)` is taken

   Sec. 8 pair (`C_micro = [-1,1]^3`, `r_in = 1`, `c_IR = 1`) is in other
   units and must not be crossed with it (Grok, repair 4). A numerical
   coincidence of speeds, or a value from a different normalization, is
   not a comparison. "Hypercube up to scale and isometry" and
   "parallelepiped" (Minkowski sum of `d` linearly independent segments)
   are checked exactly from the vertices of `C_micro`.

   parallelotope is any polytope that tiles space by translations

## 1. Two facts used throughout [hand]

**Lemma V (vertex patterns are unique).** If every `a_i ≠ 0`, each vertex
`v` of `Z = sum_i [-a_i, a_i]` is the sum of exactly one sign pattern.
Proof: `v` uniquely maximizes some linear functional `φ` with
`φ(a_i) ≠ 0` for all `i`; any pattern summing to `v` also maximizes `φ`,
which forces `ε_i = sign φ(a_i)`. Hence coincident displacements (several
patterns, one point) occur only at non-vertices, and the amplitude at a
vertex is the amplitude of a single pattern: it is nonzero **iff** the
vertex is in the certified support. In particular `conv F = Z` iff every
vertex of `Z` is in `F`.

**Lemma P (pattern amplitudes, rank-one projectors).** If factor `i` is
`sum_{s=±} Q_i^s T(s a_i)` with `Q_i^±` rank-one projectors on `C^2`
(Bloch vectors `±q_i`), the amplitude of pattern `(s_1, …, s_n)` is
`Q_n^{s_n} … Q_1^{s_1}`, nonzero iff no consecutive pair
`s_i q_i`, `s_{i+1} q_{i+1}` is antipodal; for `s_i = s_{i+1}` this means
`q_{i+1} ≠ −q_i`, for `s_i ≠ s_{i+1}` it means `q_{i+1} ≠ q_i`. For the
conjugated JDW factors `R^{-1} S R` with `R = U(0, π/2, 0, θ)` (JDW Eq. (1),
`verify_c_across_lattices_gate.py` l. 92–97), the projector of the L
component is onto `R^† |0⟩ = (−i cos θ, sin θ)`, Bloch vector
`m(θ) = (0, sin 2θ, cos 2θ)`.
