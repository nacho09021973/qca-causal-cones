## 1. Statement under audit

> **(PH) [UNVERIFIED], provisional predicate.** For `s = 2`, `d <= 3`,
> within the declared classes of Sec. 2 and only for models with an
> admissible isotropic untilted node: the ceiling is attained on a

Two directions are kept apart:

- **(PH⇐)** `C_micro` a hypercube up to scale and isometry ⇒ there exists
  an admissible walk on that substrate with `c_0 = r_in`.
- **(PH⇒)** `C_micro` not a hypercube up to scale and isometry ⇒ every
  admissible walk on that substrate has `c_0 < r_in`.

A body with no admissible isotropic untilted node in the declared class is
outside the statement: it is on neither side of the biconditional and is
recorded as `OUTSIDE_DEFINITIONS` (Sec. 3.4).

**The predicate is a geometric classification suggested by the existing
cases, not a physical explanation.** Whether saturation is controlled by
this geometry, or by algebraic and isotropy constraints that coincide with

**Zonotope reading (PS′) [UNVERIFIED], product class only, not equivalent
to (PH).** For a walk that is a product of `n` coin–partial-shift factors,
each partial shift moving by `±a_i`, the composite step set is
`F = {sum_i ε_i a_i}` and `C_micro = conv F` is the zonotope
`sum_i [-a_i, a_i]` (convex hull of a Minkowski sum is the Minkowski sum
of the hulls). It is a parallelepiped exactly when the reduced generators
(`a_i` up to sign and positive scale, parallel ones merged) are `d`
linearly independent vectors. (PS′) reads: in the product class, the
ceiling is attained iff the generators reduce to `d` independent
directions. **(PS′) is not equivalent to (PH):** `d` independent
generators need not be orthogonal or of equal length, so a parallelepiped
need not be a hypercube (e.g. a 60° rhombus in `d = 2`). No equivalence is
claimed until proved. Examples in the promoted rows: square (two
generators), BCC/cube (three orthogonal generators), `W_hex` (three
generators at 120°, `d = 2`: the hexagon). The zonotope facts it would use
(e.g. the space-tiling criterion of Shephard 1974 and McMullen 1975) are
cited by Moriyama–Otozawa (arXiv:2411.09141v1) and have not been read
here. The same domain clause applies to (PS′): a body (e.g. a rhombus)
whose declared class has no admissible isotropic untilted node is
`OUTSIDE_DEFINITIONS` and is not on the "attained" side. Phase 0 records
the reduced generators of each product-class row and gives no verdict on
(PS′); the counterexample criterion of Sec. 4 concerns (PH) only.

## 1. L0 — the ceiling `c_0 <= r_in` on `W_hex`

Let `U` be in `W_hex`. Multiplying by the scalar `e^{i phi}` does not change
the support, so take `U(0) = I`. Let `n_f` be the unit outward normal of a
facet of the regular hexagon `C = conv F`, with edge vector `d in Lambda`
(edges join lattice vertices). For a lattice vector `h = a g_1 + b g_2` and
`d = p g_1 + q g_2`,

`h . Rot90(d) = (sqrt3/2)(b p - a q)`   (verifier, check L0),

so `h . n_f = m_h delta` with `m_h in Z` and `delta = sqrt3/(2|d|)`. The
extreme values of `h . n_f` over `F` are `±h_C(n_f)` (central symmetry of
the hexagon) and are attained at vertices, which lie in `F`; hence
`h_C(n_f) = N delta` for an integer `N` and every `m_h` satisfies
`|m_h| <= N`.

For unit vectors `x, y in C^2` set `p(s) = <y, U(s n_f / delta) x>`. Then
`p(s) = sum_{|m|<=N} c_m e^{-i m s}` is a trigonometric polynomial of degree
`N` with `|p| <= 1` (U unitary). Bernstein's inequality (Lemma B below,
proved here) gives `|p'(0)| <= N`, i.e. `|<y, dU/dt(0) x>| <= N delta =
h_C(n_f)` along `n_f`. Taking the supremum over `x, y`,
`||A(n_f)|| <= h_C(n_f) = r_in`, where `A(n) = i dU(t n)/dt (0)`. `A(n_f)` is
Hermitian: differentiating `U U^dag = I` at `t = 0`, where `U = I`, shows
`dU/dt(0)` is anti-Hermitian. With `s = 2` and a traceless isotropic cone,
`A(n_f)^2 = c_0^2 I`, so the Hermitian `A(n_f)` has spectrum `{±c_0}` and
`||A(n_f)|| = c_0` (without Hermiticity, `M^2 = c_0^2 I` would not fix the
norm). Therefore **`c_0 <= r_in` for every walk in `W_hex`**. Only the
facet normal is needed, since `r_in = min h_C` is attained there.

**Lemma B (Bernstein, proved in-repo; repair R1 of the 2026-11-02 Grok
review).** If `p(s) = sum_{|m|<=N} c_m e^{-ims}` and `|p| <= 1` on `R`, then
`|p'(s_0)| <= N` for every `s_0`.

*Reduction to real polynomials.* Fix `s_0` and choose `alpha` with
`e^{i alpha} p'(s_0) = |p'(s_0)|`. `R(s) = Re(e^{i alpha} p(s))` is a real
trigonometric polynomial of degree `<= N`, `|R| <= 1`, and
`R'(s_0) = Re(e^{i alpha} p'(s_0)) = |p'(s_0)|`. So it suffices to show
`R'(s_0) <= N` for real `R`.

*Real case.* Suppose `R'(s_0) > N`. Choose `0 < eps < 1` so small that
`R_eps = (1 - eps) R` still has `R_eps'(s_0) > N`; then `|R_eps| < 1`
everywhere. Pick `beta in (-pi, 0)` with `cos beta = R_eps(s_0)` (possible
since `|R_eps(s_0)| < 1`) and set `C(s) = cos(N(s - s_0) + beta)`. Then
`C(s_0) = R_eps(s_0)` and `C'(s_0) = -N sin beta in (0, N]`, so
`D = C - R_eps` has `D(s_0) = 0`, `D'(s_0) < 0`; in particular `D` is not
identically zero. On one period `C` reaches `±1` alternately at `2N`
points `t_1 < ... < t_2N`, and there `|R_eps| < 1` gives
`sign D(t_j) = sign C(t_j)`, alternating. Hence `D` has a zero in each of
the `2N` open arcs between consecutive `t_j`. The point `s_0` is not a
`t_j` (`D(s_0) = 0 != D(t_j)`), so it lies in one arc `(t_j, t_{j+1})`, on
which `C` is monotone; `C'(s_0) > 0` makes it increasing, so
`C(t_j) = -1`, `C(t_{j+1}) = +1`, i.e. `D(t_j) < 0 < D(t_{j+1})`. Since `D`
falls through zero at `s_0` (`D'(s_0) < 0`), `D > 0` just left of `s_0` and
`D < 0` just right of it: there is a further zero in `(t_j, s_0)` and one in
`(s_0, t_{j+1})`. That arc holds at least three zeros, so `D` has at least
`2N + 2` zeros per period. But `e^{iNs} D(s)` is an algebraic polynomial of
degree `<= 2N` in `z = e^{is}`, so a nonzero `D` has at most `2N` zeros per
period. Contradiction; hence `R'(s_0) <= N`. Applying this to `R(-s)` gives
`R'(s_0) >= -N`. `square`

This is the classical argument (prior art; no novelty claimed); it is
written out so that L0 closes in-repo as the contract (Sec. 2) requires.

## 1. Two further linear consequences of saturation

Notation of the Stage A report. Suppose `c_0 = r_in = r`. Fix a facet
normal `n`, the edge direction `e = Rot90(n)`, and `x_+`, `x_-` the unit
eigenvectors of `n.sigma` (eigenvalues `+1`, `-1`). On the facet line,
`U(t n) = D(t) = e^{-irt} P_+ + e^{irt} P_-` (rigidity). Levels `lambda`
are the projections `h.n`.

**Lemma A3 (first moments).** For every layer `lambda` and both signs,
`sum_{h in lambda} (h.e) <x_±, A_h x_±> = 0`.

*Proof (sign +).* `f(z; s) = e^{irt} <x_+, U(t n + s e) x_+>` is, for fixed
real `s`, an analytic polynomial in `z = e^{i delta t}` (the exponents
`r - h.n` are `>= 0` multiples of `delta`), with `|f| <= 1` on `|z| = 1`
(unitarity) and `f(z; 0) = 1` (rigidity). Let `g(z) = d f/ds (z; 0)`, again an
analytic polynomial. From `|f|^2 = 1 + 2 s Re g + O(s^2) <= 1` for both
signs of `s`, `Re g = 0` on the circle, so `g` is an imaginary constant.
At `z = 1` (`t = 0`), `g = -i <x_+, A(e) x_+> = -i r (e.n) = 0`. Hence
`g = 0`; its Fourier coefficients are `-i sum_{h in lambda} (h.e) <x_+, A_h x_+>`.
The sign `-` is the same with `e^{-irt}` and `z̄`. ∎

**Lemma A4 (off-diagonal first order).** For every layer `lambda`,
`sum_{h in lambda} (h.e) <x_+, A_h x_-> = sum_{h in -lambda} (h.e) conj<x_-, A_h x_+>`.

*Proof.* Differentiating `U^dag U = I` along `e` on the facet line gives
that `K(t) = D(t)^dag d_e U(t n)` is anti-Hermitian. With
`d_e U = -i sum_h (h.e) A_h e^{-it h.n}`, `K_{+-} = -i sum_h (h.e) <x_+, A_h x_->
e^{it(r - h.n)}` and `conj K_{-+} = i sum_h (h.e) conj<x_-, A_h x_+>
e^{it(r + h.n)}`. `K_{+-} + conj K_{-+} = 0` for all `t`; matching the
frequency `r - lambda = r + lambda'` gives `lambda' = -lambda` and the
stated identity. (The diagonal of `K` gives `Re g = 0` only; A3 is
stronger because it uses analyticity.) ∎

A3 and A4 are consequences of unitarity, rigidity and the normal form
`A(v) = r v.sigma` (hex-ceiling report Sec. 3, Step 2; A3 uses it at
`z = 1`); they are not new assumptions. Their use is that they are linear.

## 3. Exact infeasibility certificate

**Exploration (no verdict).** Least squares on the 730 equations from 100
random starts converge to the same positive minimum (`5.056e-3`), which
suggests no real solution. A search over the 56-dimensional span for a
combination whose Gram matrix in the basis `(1, t)` is positive definite
(maximising `lambda_min` at trace 1, SLSQP, 20 starts) found
`lambda_min ≈ 3.8e-4 > 0` in every start. The best combination was rounded
to rational multipliers `c_i` (denominators `<= 10^9`) and stored in
`scripts/data/hex_s19_certificate.json`.

**Certificate (verifier, exact).** `p = sum_i c_i f_i` is formed exactly;
its unique Gram matrix `G` in the basis `(1, t)` satisfies
`p = (1,t) G (1,t)^T` identically, and exact Gaussian elimination over
`QQ(sqrt3)` gives 19 positive pivots, so `G` is positive definite. Then
`p(t) > 0` for every `t in R^18`, while at any solution every `f_i`, hence
`p`, vanishes. **Contract form: Sec. 4 (i)** (Amendment 1, Sec. 1): an
ideal element with explicit rational cofactors `c_i`, whose absence of real
zeros is certified exactly by `G > 0` (Gram identity plus 19 positive
pivots in `QQ(sqrt3)`). It is not claimed as a form (ii) certificate.
As a corollary only (not built or checked by the verifier): for
`0 < mu <= 1/(G^{-1})_00`, `G - mu e_0 e_0^T = L D L^T` with `D >= 0`, so
`-1 = sum_j (d_j/mu) l_j(t)^2 + sum_i (-c_i/mu) f_i(t)`, with coefficients
in `QQ(sqrt3)`. The system's own coefficients lie in `QQ(sqrt3)` (normal
form and hexagon geometry); the multipliers `c_i` are rational. The
docstring of `check_certificate` still says «contract Sec. 4 (ii)»; the
amendment supersedes it, and the verifier's bytes are unchanged.

**Verdict.** M1-B19 `PROVED_FOR_S19`: no s=2 walk with `F within S19`
(in `W_hex^sym`, hence in `W_hex`) reaches `c_0 = r_in`. This includes the
support of JDW L6c. HX2 `CONFIRMED_FOR_S19` (and S7, S13). The gap is
qualitative; no bound below `r_in` is claimed.
