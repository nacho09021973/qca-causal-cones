## 2. Conventions (contract Sec. 2)

- Moving amplitude by `h` carries `exp(-i k.h)`; an operator that reads the
  value at `X+v` moves amplitude by `-v`.
- In every product the factor with the smallest index acts first, as the
  sources write explicitly for JDW Eqs. (4), (6) and ADMP Eq. (10). For the
  `prod_j` notation of JDW Eqs. (2), (8) the same reading is a declared
  choice.
- One complete update = one time unit; positions are divided by the
  longest `|h|` in the composite step set `F`.
- Substrate = translation group generated by `F` together with `F`, as
  computed from the equation, not the paper's lattice label.

**Control against the sources.** Converting each `c_0` back to the source's
own units reproduces the continuum speed each source states: JDW
`Delta t = 3 epsilon/2` (L6c, L6d), `epsilon` (L6e), `3 sqrt3 epsilon/4`
(L6f) give `c = 1`; ADMP, Farrelly–Short and Mlodinow–Brun give `c = 1` per
update in their units. This checks the routing and scale conventions. It
does **not** test the product order: since every factor satisfies
`W_j(0) = I`, the linear term is the sum of the factors' derivatives and is
order-independent (Grok review). The `prod_j` order of JDW Eqs. (2), (8)
remains a declared reading; `K` (Sec. 5) and vertex coefficients of L6c and
L6f may depend on it.

**Fourier sign.** Relative to JDW's plane wave `exp i(omega t - k.X)`, the
encoded symbol corresponds to `k -> -k`. Every tabulated quantity
(`U(0)`, quadratic cone, Gram, Hessian) is even in `k`, so the table is
unaffected.

## 5. Massive rows (Part 3)

Mass parameter `theta` = quasienergy gap at `k=0`, as each source inserts
it: MB coin `e^{-i theta Q}` (L5b); JDW `U_m = U(0,0,-pi/2,phi)
= e^{-i phi sigma_x}` on the left, `theta = 3m/2` (L6c, and L6d "the same
way", JDW p.2), `theta = 3 sqrt3 m/4` (L6f); JDW L6e replaces `U^1` so that
`U(0) = e^{-i m sigma_x}`, `theta = m`.

Method (exact, no matrix-log series). For `U(0) = e^{-i theta Q}`,
`Q^2 = I`, Duhamel's formula gives
`i dU U(0)^dag = X_par + (sin theta/theta) X_perp e^{i theta Q}`, with
`X_par`, `X_perp` the parts of `dH` commuting and anticommuting with `Q`.
(The reviewed version omitted the unitary factor `e^{i theta Q}`; both
reviewers flagged it; corrected here.) The verifier forms
`B_a = X_par + X_perp e^{i theta Q}` and asserts `X_par = 0`; then `B_a` is
Hermitian, anticommutes with `Q`, and `{B_a, B_b} = {X_a, X_b}`, so its Gram
matrix is that of `dH`. `c_H(n̂)^2 = n̂.g.n̂` with
`{dH_a, dH_b} = 2 g_ab I` — the linear spatial coefficient of `H = i log U`
at fixed mass, as in the Weyl/Dirac gate. No tabulated number changes. For `omega`,
`G = (U+U^dag)/2` has eigenvalues `cos omega_j`, is scalar at `k=0` and has
zero gradient, so its Hessian gives `omega_j^2 = theta^2 +
(theta/sin theta) q_j |k|^2`. The script certifies
`Hess cos(omega)(0) = -c_0^2 cos(theta) I + sin(theta) K` with `K`
independent of `theta`; an isotropic `c_omega2` exists iff `K = 0`, and then
`c_omega2 = c_0 sqrt(theta cot theta)`.

**Positive control.** On the reviewed minimal 1D Dirac block of the
Weyl/Dirac gate the same code returns `c_H = c_0 M cot M` and `K = 0`
(isotropic `c_omega2`), reproducing `reports/2026-09-14--weyl-dirac-exact-gate.md`
Secs. 5–6.

| Row | linear term at `theta != 0` | `omega^2` curvature at `theta != 0` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L5b (MB, BCC, s=4) | isotropic, `c_H = c_0 theta/sin theta` | `K != 0`: anisotropic and band-split; no isotropic `c_omega2` |
| L6c (JDW) | isotropic, `c_H = c_0 theta/sin theta` | `K = diag(-5 sqrt3/32, 3 sqrt3/32)`: anisotropic |
| L6d (JDW) | isotropic, `c_H = c_0 theta/sin theta` | `K = [[sqrt3/16, -3/8], [-3/8, -3 sqrt3/16]]`: anisotropic |
| L6f (JDW) | isotropic, `c_H = c_0 theta/sin theta` | `K = diag(-3 sqrt3/16, 5 sqrt3/16)`: anisotropic |
| L6e (JDW) | **anisotropic**: `g / (c_0 theta/sin theta)^2 = [[1, sin 2theta], [sin 2theta, 1]]` | `K = [[0, -1/2], [-1/2, 0]]`: anisotropic |
| L6a (ADMP) | OPEN: mass enters inside the coin, `U(0) = prod_i e^{-i mu tau_i}`; not encoded | OPEN |
| control: 1D Dirac | `c_H = c_0 M cot M` | `K = 0`, `c_omega2 = c_0 sqrt(M cot M)` |

Readings, each limited to these rows:

1. Where the mass is a separate coin multiplying a unitary hopping product
   (L5b, L6c, L6d, L6f), `c_H = c_0 theta/sin theta > c_0` for
   `0 < theta < pi`. In the standard Dirac family, where the hopping
   amplitude is scaled by `cos M`, `c_H = c_0 M cot M < c_0`. So `c_H` can
   exceed the ceiling `r_in` in these rows. This contradicts no bound: `c_H`
   is a Hamiltonian coefficient, not a propagation speed.
2. In every massive row computed here, `omega^2` has **no isotropic
   quadratic coefficient** at finite mass; the anisotropy is
   `O(sin theta)` and vanishes as `theta -> 0`, where `c_H`, and the
   isotropic part of the curvature, both return to `c_0`. The standard
   Dirac family is the only computed case with an isotropic `c_omega2`.
   The repo rule "do not treat `c_H` and `c_{omega^2}` as interchangeable"
   is strengthened here: at finite mass there may be no single
   `c_{omega^2}` at all.
3. L6e shows that where the mass is inserted matters: replacing an
   internal coin of the sequence makes the linear term itself anisotropic.

## 7. What this does not establish

- No rigidity, classification or general QCA law: the rows are a declared
  finite list from five sources, plus the repo's existing rows.
- Not that hexagonal substrates cannot reach the ceiling: only that none of
  the four declared hexagonal walks does.
- No statement about doublers or nodes other than `k=0`; no
  finite-momentum dispersion beyond second order; no `V_grp^max`.
- No claim about which P17 hypothesis each L6 row violates beyond the
  sources' own account (ADMP p.2: spinors on triangle edges). Identifying
  it is left OPEN; the substrate result (triangular group) is a candidate
  explanation, not a proof.
- No novelty claim. Bernstein and the sources' constructions are prior
  art. No claim about our universe.
- Convention dependence: the JDW `prod_j` order for Eqs. (2) and (8) is a
  declared reading. The agreement with each source's continuum speed (Sec. 2)
  supports the conventions but does not prove them.

QCA law may be drawn from it (PROJECT_INSTRUCTIONS §7).

sums of one displacement per partial shift, with internal components
routed as the equation states. If two rows labelled differently (e.g.
"honeycomb" and "triangular") yield the same group and `F` up to

walks, and must be reported that way. Computing `F` from the equation is
the first execution step of each row; a disagreement with the paper's

## 3. Quantifiers frozen per row

For each row, before computing: internal dimension `s`, group and action,
representation of the isotropy group (irreducible or not), node and sector,
any irreducibility or minimality assumption. Every conclusion keeps them.

P17 compatibility: L6 rows are two-component walks on lattices that P17
p.7 Prop. 5 excludes for isotropic `s=2` QWs, so each violates at least
one P17 hypothesis. Before computing, record the source's own account
(ADMP p.2: spinors on edges shared by triangles; JDW: none stated, to be
recorded as such). Identifying which hypothesis actually fails is an
execution item, reported per row; it is not a new theorem.

## 4. Quantities per row

All exact (SymPy, zero tolerance):

1. `C_micro = conv F`, support function `h_C`, inradius `r_in`,
   `V_causal^max`.
2. Exact Bloch symbol and eigenphases at the declared node.
3. `c_0` (massless linear IR coefficient) and, if the node cone is not
   isotropic, the directional coefficient `c_0(n̂)`.
4. `C_infty^strict` by vertex-trace survival, hence `c_strict^iso`.
   If survival cannot be decided exactly, the entry is `OPEN`, not
   estimated.
5. `S(n̂) = c_0 / h_C(n̂)` and `S_min`.
6. `V_grp^max = sup |∇omega|` if it is exactly certifiable; else `OPEN`.
7. If the source defines a mass term: `c_H` and `c_{omega^2}` separately.

## 5. Hypotheses, stated before solving

- **Ha.** In every declared `s=2` row with a massless node, `c_0 = r_in`.
- **Hb.** In every declared row, `c_strict^iso = r_in` (all vertex

- **Hc.** No declared row has `c_0 > c_strict^iso`. For rows outside N3
  this is a check, not a consequence of point (3).

Refuting any of them is a valid outcome. A pattern across rows is
recorded as a pattern in the declared list, not as a law.

## 1. Single question

> On the hexagonal substrate, is there a two-component walk of the class

## 2. Declared class

`W_hex` exactly as in `HEX_CEILING_GATE.md` Sec. 1 (triangular lattice
`Lambda`, `s = 2`, product of coin–partial-shift factors, `C_micro = conv F`
a regular hexagon from the exact composite support, scalar `U(0)`,
traceless isotropic linear term, longest vector of `F` of length 1).

Two readings of the class are kept apart:

- **Gap direction.** A proof that `c_0 < r_in` may use only unitarity, `F`,
  the node and isotropy (as the minimal-support T2 did). It then holds for
  the larger class `W_hex^sym` of all s=2 unitary Laurent polynomials with
  those properties, which contains `W_hex`; it is stated for `W_hex^sym`.
- **Witness direction.** A witness must lie in `W_hex`: it is given as an
  explicit product `C_n D_n(k) ... C_1 D_1(k)`. A saturating symbol found
  without a product factorization is reported as a witness in `W_hex^sym`
  only, which does not meet result (a); in two dimensions factorization is
  not automatic [UNVERIFIED as a general statement; not used].

**Supports.** Regular lattice hexagons centred at the origin have vertices
`R^j v`, `v in Lambda`, `R` the 60° rotation. Ordered by the number of
lattice points of the closed hull (to be certified by the verifier, not
assumed):

- `S7`: `v = g_1` — 7 points; every walk is of minimal type (done in T2).
- `S13`: `v = g_1 + g_2` — origin, inner ring of six unit vectors, six
  vertices: 13 points. Smallest support not covered by T2.
- `S19`: `v = 2 g_1` — the radius-2 ball (contains JDW L6c, `c_0/r_in =
  sqrt3/2`).
- larger hexagons: covered only by a structural argument (Sec. 4, Stage A).

## 4. Method, named before any computation

**Stage A — structural reduction (by hand, checked by the verifier).**

1. Facet-line rigidity (hex-ceiling report Sec. 3, Step 1, reused): along
   each of the three facet-normal lines the top edge sums to `P_+(n_f)`,
   the bottom edge to `P_-(n_f)`, every intermediate layer to `0`.
2. Extreme-face identities. The Newton polytope of `U U^dag` lies in
   `C - C = 2C`. For each edge `e` of `C` with outward normal `n_e`, the
   coefficients of `U U^dag` and `U^dag U` on the face of `2C` in direction
   `n_e` vanish (except at `0`, not on the face). With `E_e(x)` the edge
   polynomial of the top edge and `E_{-e}(x)` that of the opposite edge,
   this gives `E_e(x) E_{-e}(x)^dag = 0` and `E_{-e}(x)^dag E_e(x) = 0` as
   one-variable Laurent identities, together with `E_e(1) = P_+(n_e)`,
   `E_{-e}(1) = P_-(n_e)` from step 1.
3. Use 1–2 (and, if needed, the next-to-extreme faces) to restrict the
   edge coefficients to rank-one forms and eliminate unknowns, before any
   solver is run. Step 2 is a standard fact about Laurent polynomials
   (product of extreme coefficients); its use here is stated and proved in
   the report, not cited.

**Stage B — exact decision on a bounded support (`S13`, then `S19`).**

- Unknowns: the coefficients left after Stage A and the normal form of the
  hex-ceiling report Sec. 3, Step 2 (`A(n) = r_in n . sigma`, vertices at
  `k pi/3`).
- Equations: layer equations, node and isotropy, and unitarity
  `sum_h A_{h+d} A_h^dag = delta_{d0} I`, `sum_h A_h^dag A_{h+d} = delta_{d0} I`.
- **Infeasibility certificate (gap).** Accepted forms only, each checked
  exactly and quickly by the verifier without rerunning a solver:
  (i) a polynomial `p` in the ideal, given with explicit exact cofactors
  `p = sum_i h_i f_i`, whose absence of real zeros is itself certified
  exactly (e.g. a univariate polynomial with negative discriminant, as in
  T2, or a sum-of-squares-plus-positive-constant representation of `p`);
  or (ii) for the real system `f_1 = ... = f_m = 0`, an identity
  `-1 = sum_j q_j(x)^2 + sum_i h_i(x) f_i(x)` with exact rational
  coefficients, the verifier expanding both sides and checking equality. A Gröbner basis over `C`
  different from `{1}` decides nothing about real solvability and is not a
  verdict.
- **Witness.** Closed form, certified exactly: unitarity, composite `F`,
  regular hexagonal hull, node, isotropy, `c_0 = r_in`, and the product
  factorization (Sec. 2).
- Exploratory numerics (a versioned script labelled exploratory) may guide
  the search for a witness or for the certificate. **No numerical result,
  scan, optimisation or failed search enters a verdict**, and no rigidity
  is inferred from one.

## 5. Stop rules and budget

- Order: Stage A, then M1-B13, then M1-B19. Do not start the next stage
  before the previous has a verdict or a recorded stop.
- Budget: one working session, and at most 3600 s of solver wall time
  **cumulative per support** (`S13`, `S19`), however it is split across
  runs; every run is recorded with its command, wall time and exit code.
  A run cut by the budget is inconclusive and is not evidence.
- Stop at the first of: a witness meeting Sec. 4 (result (a); then M1
  closes, with the limited reading of Sec. 0); a gap proof for `W_hex^sym` (line kept); budget exhausted
  (result (b), `OPEN_AFTER_<analysis>` naming the stages done).
- Do not widen the class during execution; restricted results stay
  restricted.

## 6. Exclusions

walks, new substrate classes, general QCA laws, claims about our universe.
No novelty claim: Bernstein's inequality and its equality case are prior
art; the hexagonal-ceiling prior-art check (plan step 4) is not finished.
