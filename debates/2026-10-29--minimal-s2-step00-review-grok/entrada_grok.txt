## 1. Question

Units and lattice:
- `N_3 = {ε/sqrt(3) : ε ∈ {±1}^3}` (normalized, as in the predecessors). Displacements are
  labelled by their `ε` differences.
- The generated lattice is BCC, `Λ = (1/sqrt(3)) {m ∈ Z^3 : m_1 ≡ m_2 ≡ m_3 mod 2}`.
- With the convention `e^{-ik.h}`, its reciprocal lattice is
  `Λ^* = π sqrt(3) {m ∈ Z^3 : m_1 + m_2 + m_3 even}`, and the Brillouin torus is `R^3 / Λ^*`.
- In these units the Pavia baseline value is `1/sqrt(3)`. With unnormalized `ε` both velocities
  scale by `sqrt(3)` and the baseline becomes `1`; the equality is unaffected (A-R1-02).

Main question:

\[
\forall \mathcal C,\ \forall U \in \mathcal C,\ \forall (k_\star,\omega_\star)\ \text{admissible}:\quad
c_0(U,k_\star) = c_{\rm strict}^{\rm iso}(U)\ ?
\]

- A class `𝒞` is a tuple `(P, G, S_±, [R])` in family P, or `(F, G, [R])` in family F. Family F
  has no partition (A-R2-01).
- `(k_star, ω_star)` ranges over **every** admissible node and eigenphase of `U`. The sector is
  the full two-band space, and the local quasienergy branch is the one fixed by the predecessor's
  Sec. 3.
- One admissible tuple with `c_0 != c_strict^iso` is a counterexample. Its direction is recorded
  (A-R2-04).
  - Where the general ceiling is certified for that class and node (Step 0.4), only
    `c_0 < c_strict^iso` is possible.
  - Where it is not certified, for example in the `C4` face class, both directions are admitted.
  - A `c_0 > c_strict^iso` witness inside a certified class would contradict the ceiling. It is
    recorded as a conflict and reviewed before anything else.

Subsidiary questions:
- **Q-a.** Does every symbol of a class **with at least one admissible node** satisfy P17
  Eq. (2)? User decision, A-R2-05. In family F, Eq. (2) is interpreted with `h ∈ N_3`
  (`S_+ = S_− = N_3` for this purpose only, as in P17 p.3).
- **Q-b.** Does the `A±,B±` certificate transport along every listed P17 equivalence?

## Method

**Group.**
- `O` is the set of 24 proper signed permutation matrices. All arithmetic is with integers.
- Every subgroup of `S4 ≅ O` is generated by at most two elements, so closing all ordered pairs
  gives every subgroup. The certificate finds 30, the known number for `S4`.

**Family P.**
- All 30 subgroups are crossed with all 16 choices of `S_+`, keeping the pairs where `G`
  stabilizes `S_+` and is transitive on it.
- Classes are taken up to `O`-conjugation of the pair.
- The 16 choices of `S_+` are 2 tetrahedra, 6 faces and 8 "vertex plus three neighbours" sets.

**Family F.** The subgroups transitive on all eight vertices, up to conjugation.

**Representations.**
- A 2-dimensional projective unitary `R`, up to projective equivalence `R' = c W R W^†`, is a
  homomorphism `ρ: G → PU(2) ≅ SO(3)` up to `SO(3)` conjugation. Covariance
  `T_{gh} = R_g T_h R_g^†` depends only on `ρ`.
- Every image type that occurs (1, `C2`, `C3`, `C4`, `V4`, `D3`, `D4`, `A4`, `S4`) is
  `SO(3)`-conjugate to a subgroup of `O`. This is the standard classification of finite subgroups
  of `SO(3)`, cited and not re-derived; `[UNVERIFIED]` against a primary source in the repo.
  Enumerating homomorphisms into `O` is therefore exhaustive.
- Two classes are identified iff their 3-dimensional characters agree. Equal real characters
  give orthogonal equivalence, and replacing `M` by `−M` makes it `SO(3)`.
- `R` is reducible iff the trivial multiplicity of `ρ` is at least 1, meaning a fixed axis.
- The projective kernel of `R` is `ker ρ`.

**Kernel lemma (A1, A6, A7).**
- If `R` is scalar on `K = ker ρ`, then `T` is constant on `K`-orbits.
- Take a shift `ℓ != 0` whose pairs `(h, h+ℓ)` all lie inside `K`-orbits. Its unitarity identity
  `Σ T_h^† T_{h+ℓ} = 0` becomes a sum of positive semidefinite terms `T_h^† T_h`.
- So every `T_h` that appears vanishes, which contradicts `T_h != 0`.
- The certificate prints the shift and the pairs for every emptied class.

**A3, A8.**
- The shift `(2,2,2)` has the single pair `((−1,−1,−1), (1,1,1))`.
- Each shift `2h` has the single pair `(−h, h)`. With `T_{−h}^† T_h = 0` and both nonzero, both
  have rank one.
- Cayley–Hamilton, `T^2 = tr(T) T − det(T) I`, is checked symbolically.

**Controls.**
- Negative: the Pauli class `(V4, tetrahedron, faithful)` gets no certificate.
- Positive: both Pavia chiralities satisfy all unitarity identities with all eight `T_h != 0`.
  This reuses the step-01 extraction read-only.

"""Shared helpers for exact symbolic certificates.

Structural equality (`a == b`) on SymPy expressions compares syntax trees, not
mathematical values: `simplify` may return an algebraically equal but
differently written form. Two certificates (W2, W5) failed under the pinned
SymPy 1.14.0 for this reason (reports/2026-10-22--escape-corruption-audit.md).
Use `sym_zero` / `assert_sym_equal` for value identities.
"""

import sympy as sp


def sym_zero(expr):
    """True iff expr simplifies to exactly zero (trig/exp forms normalized)."""
    if isinstance(expr, sp.MatrixBase):
        return all(sym_zero(e) for e in expr)
    e = sp.expand(sp.sympify(expr).rewrite(sp.exp))
    return sp.simplify(e) == 0


def assert_sym_equal(a, b, what=""):
    """Assert a and b are equal as values; report the residual on failure."""
    residual = (sp.Matrix(a) - sp.Matrix(b)) if isinstance(a, sp.MatrixBase) \
        else sp.sympify(a) - sp.sympify(b)
    assert sym_zero(residual), f"{what}: nonzero residual {sp.simplify(residual)}"
