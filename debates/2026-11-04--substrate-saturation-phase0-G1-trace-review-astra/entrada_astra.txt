## 1. Two facts used throughout [hand]

**Lemma V (vertex patterns are unique).** If every `a_i ≠ 0`, each vertex
`v` of `Z = sum_i [-a_i, a_i]` is the sum of exactly one sign pattern.
Proof: `v` uniquely maximizes some linear functional `φ` with
`φ(a_i) ≠ 0` for all `i`; any pattern summing to `v` also maximizes `φ`,
which forces `ε_i = sign φ(a_i)`. Hence coincident displacements (several
patterns, one point) occur only at non-vertices, and the amplitude at a
vertex is the amplitude of a single pattern: it is nonzero **iff** the
vertex is in the certified support. In particular `conv F = Z` iff every
vertex of `Z` is in `F`.

**Lemma P (pattern amplitudes, rank-one projectors).** If factor `i` is
`sum_{s=±} Q_i^s T(s a_i)` with `Q_i^±` rank-one projectors on `C^2`
(Bloch vectors `±q_i`), the amplitude of pattern `(s_1, …, s_n)` is
`Q_n^{s_n} … Q_1^{s_1}`, nonzero iff no consecutive pair
`s_i q_i`, `s_{i+1} q_{i+1}` is antipodal; for `s_i = s_{i+1}` this means
`q_{i+1} ≠ −q_i`, for `s_i ≠ s_{i+1}` it means `q_{i+1} ≠ q_i`. For the
conjugated JDW factors `R^{-1} S R` with `R = U(0, π/2, 0, θ)` (JDW Eq. (1),
`verify_c_across_lattices_gate.py` l. 92–97), the projector of the L
component is onto `R^† |0⟩ = (−i cos θ, sin θ)`, Bloch vector
`m(θ) = (0, sin 2θ, cos 2θ)`.

`F = {sum_i ε_i a_i}` and `C_micro = conv F` is the zonotope
`sum_i [-a_i, a_i]` (convex hull of a Minkowski sum is the Minkowski sum
of the hulls). It is a parallelepiped exactly when the reduced generators
(`a_i` up to sign and positive scale, parallel ones merged) are `d`
linearly independent vectors. (PS′) reads: in the product class, the
ceiling is attained iff the generators reduce to `d` independent
directions. **(PS′) is not equivalent to (PH):** `d` independent
generators need not be orthogonal or of equal length, so a parallelepiped
need not be a hypercube (e.g. a 60° rhombus in `d = 2`). No equivalence is
claimed until proved. Examples in the promoted rows: square (two
generators), BCC/cube (three orthogonal generators), `W_hex` (three
generators at 120°, `d = 2`: the hexagon). The zonotope facts it would use
(e.g. the space-tiling criterion of Shephard 1974 and McMullen 1975) are
cited by Moriyama–Otozawa (arXiv:2411.09141v1) and have not been read
here. The same domain clause applies to (PS′): a body (e.g. a rhombus)
whose declared class has no admissible isotropic untilted node is
`OUTSIDE_DEFINITIONS` and is not on the "attained" side. Phase 0 records
the reduced generators of each product-class row and gives no verdict on
(PS′); the counterexample criterion of Sec. 4 concerns (PH) only.

## 2. Quantifiers, domain and normalization

1. **Quantifiers.** "The ceiling is attained on a substrate" means: there
   exists at least one admissible walk `W` on that substrate with
   `c_0(W) = r_in(C_W)`. It does not mean that every walk saturates. A
   non-saturating walk on a substrate where another walk saturates is not
   a counterexample to (PH⇐) (e.g. L3s4 on BCC, which in any case has
   `s = 4` and is outside the domain).
2. **Domain.** `s = 2`, `d <= 3`, models with an admissible isotropic
   untilted node, and only the classes already declared in the frozen
   contracts the promoted rows cite (C_ACROSS_LATTICES_GATE,
   HEX_CEILING_GATE, HEX_SUPREMUM_GATE and the row contracts).
   **"Admissible"** means exactly: the walk belongs to one of these declared
   classes and has an isotropic untilted node at `k = 0` in the sense of
   Sec. 2.4 (`U(0)` scalar, traceless Hermitian linear term with
   `{T_a, T_b} = 2 c_0^2 delta_ab`, zero tilt; equivalently
   `A(n)^2 = c_0^2 |n|^2 I`, HEX_CEILING_GATE Sec. 1). It is not the
   "admissible graph" of P17, whose isotropy in its own sense excludes the
   triangular lattice. Rows with
   `s != 2` are recorded and are outside the domain: L5b and L3s4
   (`s = 4`, c-across-lattices report Sec. 4 table); L6b (`s = 6`: L6b
   report Secs. 1–2, `s = 6` counted per cell, three edges with `C^2`
   each, whereas the parent's `s = 2` counts per edge; the row is promoted
   with `s = 6` by L6B_TRIANGULAR_ROW, described in CS l. 88–94 as «a
   revision of `C_ACROSS_LATTICES` restricted to row L6b», and CS l. 106
   records «`s = 6`, not the parent's `s = 2`»). The parent contract
   C_ACROSS_LATTICES_GATE and the c-across-lattices table were not amended
   and still list L6b with `s = 2` (`OPEN`, not encoded); the exclusion
   rests on the promoted `s = 6`. No extrapolation to other `s`, `d`,
   lattices or classes.
3. **Substrate.** As in C_ACROSS_LATTICES_GATE Sec. 2: the translation group
   generated by one complete update together with its composite step set.
   Here `N := F`, the composite step set of one complete update (all sums
   of one displacement per partial shift), not the displacement set of a
   single partial shift. `0 ∈ N` does not change `C_micro`, `r_in` or the
   longest step when it is interior.
4. **Normalization and single source per row.** Every row is compared with
   the same frozen definitions (CS Sec. 2): `C_micro := conv(N)`; `h_C`;
   `r_in(C_micro) := min h_C`; `c_0` the isotropic untilted cone speed at
   the massless node (`{T_a, T_b} = 2 c_0^2 delta_ab`, as certified per row
   in the c-across-lattices report Sec. 4); units: longest step 1, one
   update per time unit. **For each row the pair `(r_in, c_0)` is taken
   from exactly one source, in these units.** For L4 that source is the
   c-across-lattices report Sec. 4 table (`r_in = c_0 = 1/sqrt3`); that
   table's label "cube `[-1,1]^3`" names the shape in CS Sec. 8 units, and
   its vertices are not to be combined with the rescaled radius `1/sqrt3`
   (the rescaled cube has longest step 1). The CS
   Sec. 8 pair (`C_micro = [-1,1]^3`, `r_in = 1`, `c_IR = 1`) is in other
   units and must not be crossed with it (Grok, repair 4). A numerical
   coincidence of speeds, or a value from a different normalization, is
   not a comparison. "Hypercube up to scale and isometry" and
   "parallelepiped" (Minkowski sum of `d` linearly independent segments)
   are checked exactly from the vertices of `C_micro`.
   **Terminology.** Revision 1 said "parallelotope"; in standard use a
   parallelotope is any polytope that tiles space by translations
   (Moriyama–Otozawa, arXiv:2411.09141v1, Sec. 2.2, p. 11), and the
   regular hexagon is one. That word is not used for the predicate.
5. **Pointwise against uniform gap.** (PH⇒) is pointwise:
   `for all W, c_0(W) < r_in(C_W)`. It does not imply a uniform gap
   `sup_W c_0(W)/r_in(C_W) < 1` over a class. Phase 0 makes no claim about
   a uniform gap. T1 (`sqrt3/2`) is a uniform statement for the `n = 3`
   antipodal subclass only; M1-B13 and M1-B19 are qualitative gaps with no
   bound below `r_in`.
6. **Background bound.** `c_0 <= r_in` (L0, Bernstein) holds on the
   declared classes; a row with `c_0 > r_in` would contradict L0, not (PH).
   No such row exists.

## 4. Counterexample criterion

An exact counterexample among the promoted rows is any of:

- (against PH⇒) a substrate in the domain whose `C_micro` is not a
  hypercube up to scale and isometry, with a promoted walk with
  `c_0 = r_in` exactly;
- (against PH⇐) a substrate in the domain whose `C_micro` is a hypercube up
  to scale and isometry, on which a promoted exact result shows that no
  admissible walk of the declared class attains `c_0 = r_in`.

A failed search, a non-saturating single walk, a gap on a subclass or a
bounded support, or a row outside the domain or the definitions is not a
counterexample.

def laurent_adjoint(matrix, zs):
    """Adjoint on the unit torus: conjugate coefficients, z -> 1/z."""
    sub = {sp.conjugate(z): 1 / z for z in zs}
    return matrix.applyfunc(lambda e: sp.conjugate(sp.expand(e)).subs(sub)).T


def laurent_support(matrix, zs, bound=8):
    """Displacements h (integer generator coordinates) with nonzero
    coefficient matrix, for a symbol written with z^h <-> exp(-i k.h)."""
    shift = sp.prod([z ** bound for z in zs])
    coefficients = {}
    for r in range(matrix.rows):
        for c in range(matrix.cols):
            expr = sp.expand(shift * matrix[r, c])
            if expr == 0:
                continue
            poly = sp.Poly(expr, *zs)
            for powers, coeff in poly.terms():
                h = tuple(p - bound for p in powers)
                coefficients.setdefault(h, sp.zeros(matrix.rows, matrix.cols))
                coefficients[h][r, c] += coeff
    return {h: m for h, m in coefficients.items() if not is_zero_matrix(m)}

           is_zero_matrix(product - sp.eye(symbol.rows)))


def lattice_index(vectors):
    """|det| of the integer lattice spanned by integer vectors in Z^d,
    relative to the generator lattice (1 = they generate all of it)."""
    d = len(vectors[0])
    m = sp.Matrix(vectors).T
    hnf_rank = m.rank()
    if hnf_rank < d:
        return 0
    from sympy.matrices.normalforms import smith_normal_form
    snf = smith_normal_form(m, domain=sp.ZZ)
    return sp.prod([abs(snf[i, i]) for i in range(d)])

    S = (sp.exp(sp.I * sp.pi / 3) * sp.diag(sp.exp(-sp.I * sp.pi / 3), sp.exp(sp.I * sp.pi / 3))
         ).applyfunc(lambda e: sp.expand_complex(sp.expand(e)))  # radicals: diag(1, e^{2 pi i/3})
    record("L6a: S cubes to the identity (p.3)", is_zero_matrix(S ** 3 - I2))
    signs = {}
    # Half-angle radicals of alpha = sign*arccos(sqrt5/3), so that every
    # entry is a real radical and .H simplifies exactly.
    c_half = sp.sqrt((1 + sp.sqrt(5) / 3) / 2)
    s_half = sp.sqrt((1 - sp.sqrt(5) / 3) / 2)

    def ry(sign):
        return sp.Matrix([[c_half, -sign * s_half], [sign * s_half, c_half]])

    for sign in (1, -1):
        U0 = ry(sign)
        signs[sign] = is_zero_matrix(U0 * tau[0] * U0.H - SZ)
    print(f"  L6a: sigma_z = R_y(alpha) tau_0 R_y(alpha)^dag holds for "
          f"alpha=+arccos(sqrt5/3): {signs[1]}, alpha=-arccos(sqrt5/3): {signs[-1]}")
    record("L6a: SOURCE SIGN ISSUE recorded: Eq. (8) holds for alpha = -arccos(sqrt5/3) "
           "only, not the printed +arccos(sqrt5/3)", (not signs[1]) and signs[-1])
    U0 = ry(1 if signs[1] else -1)
    printed = [U0, U0 * S, U0 * S * S]
    printed_ok = all(is_zero_matrix(Ui.H * SZ * Ui - t) for Ui, t in zip(printed, tau))
    swapped = is_zero_matrix(printed[1].H * SZ * printed[1] - tau[2])
    print(f"  L6a: with the printed S, U_i^dag sigma_z U_i = tau_i: {printed_ok}; "
          f"U_0 S gives tau_2 instead: {swapped}")
    record("L6a: SOURCE SIGN ISSUE recorded: printed S orders the tau_i backwards",
           (not printed_ok) and swapped)
    S = S.H  # use S^{-1}; Eq. (5) with C1-C2 is the primary definition
    Us = [U0, U0 * S, U0 * S * S]
    record("L6a: with S^{-1}, tau_i = U_i^dag sigma_z U_i for i = 0, 1, 2",
           all(is_zero_matrix(Ui.H * SZ * Ui - t) for Ui, t in zip(Us, tau)))
    W = U0 * S * U0.H
    Tp = [monomial(zs, ui) * PROJ_L + monomial(zs, neg(ui)) * PROJ_R for ui in u]
    encoded = sp.expand(W * Tp[2] * W * Tp[1] * W * Tp[0])
    # Eq. (7) -> (10): U_{i+1} U_i^dag = W and U_2^dag = U_0^dag W (S^3 = I),
    # hence prod_i U_i^dag T_i U_i = U_0^dag (W T2 W T1 W T0) U_0.
    record("L6a: Eq. (7) symbol = U_0^dag (Eq. (10) symbol) U_0 exactly",
           is_zero_matrix(sp.expand(U0 * symbol * U0.H) - encoded))
    support = laurent_support(symbol, zs)
    gens = [(1, 0), (sp.Rational(1, 2), sp.sqrt(3) / 2)]
    report_row("L6a", support, gens)
    return symbol, zs, support, gens

def jdw_shift(zs, read_L, read_R):
    """(S Psi)(X) = (psi^L(X+read_L), psi^R(X+read_R)): L moves by -read_L."""
    return monomial(zs, neg(read_L)) * PROJ_L + monomial(zs, neg(read_R)) * PROJ_R

# Massless geometry and speeds at the declared node k = 0.
#
# Normalisation (contract Sec. 2): Cartesian positions divided by the longest
# |h| in F, one complete update per time unit. U(k) is obtained from the
# Laurent symbol by z_j = exp(sigma * i * k.g_j / L), sigma = -1 for the
# symbols built above (z <-> exp(-i k.g)) and +1 for the published standard
# symbols (z = exp(i k)). Signs of nonzero algebraic numbers are decided by
# SymPy's exact relational evaluation on radicals; zero tests are symbolic.

def positive(x):
    v = sp.radsimp(sp.simplify(x))
    if v == 0:
        return 0
    return 1 if bool(v > 0) else -1


def hull(points):
    """Facets (unit outward normal, distance) and vertex set of conv(points),
    for points spanning R^d with the origin in the interior."""
    import itertools
    d = points[0].rows
    facets = {}
    for combo in itertools.combinations(range(len(points)), d):
        base = points[combo[0]]
        if d == 1:
            normal = sp.Matrix([1])
        else:
            diff = sp.Matrix([list((points[i] - base).T) for i in combo[1:]])
            ns = diff.nullspace()
            if len(ns) != 1:
                continue
            normal = ns[0]
        offset = (normal.T * base)[0]
        if offset == 0 or positive(offset) == 0:
            continue
        if positive(offset) < 0:
            normal, offset = -normal, -offset
        if all(positive(offset - (normal.T * q)[0]) >= 0 for q in points):
            norm = sp.sqrt((normal.T * normal)[0])
            unit = (normal / norm).applyfunc(lambda e: sp.radsimp(sp.simplify(e)))
            facets[tuple(unit)] = sp.radsimp(sp.simplify(offset / norm))
    verts = []
    for idx, q in enumerate(points):
        on = [sp.Matrix(n) for n, dist in facets.items()
              if positive((sp.Matrix(n).T * q)[0] - dist) == 0]
        if on and sp.Matrix.hstack(*on).rank() == d:
            verts.append(idx)
    return facets, verts


def node_speeds(symbol, zs, gens, scale, sigma, dim):
    k = sp.symbols(f"k1:{dim + 1}", real=True)
    subs = {}
    for z, g in zip(zs, gens):
        phase = sum((k[a] * g[a] for a in range(dim)), 0) / scale
        subs[z] = sp.exp(sigma * sp.I * phase)
    U = symbol.subs(subs)
    at0 = U.subs({ka: 0 for ka in k}).applyfunc(lambda e: sp.radsimp(sp.simplify(e)))
    s_dim = symbol.rows
    A = [(sp.I * U.diff(ka)).subs({kb: 0 for kb in k}).applyfunc(
        lambda e: sp.radsimp(sp.simplify(sp.expand_complex(e)))) for ka in k]
    tilt = [sp.simplify(Ai.trace() / s_dim) for Ai in A]
    T = [Ai - t * sp.eye(s_dim) for Ai, t in zip(A, tilt)]
    c2 = sp.radsimp(sp.simplify((T[0] * T[0])[0, 0]))
    iso = all(is_zero_matrix(T[a] * T[a] - c2 * sp.eye(s_dim)) for a in range(dim)) and all(
        is_zero_matrix(T[a] * T[b] + T[b] * T[a]) for a in range(dim) for b in range(a + 1, dim))
    herm = all(is_zero_matrix(Ai - Ai.H) for Ai in A)
    return at0, A, tilt, c2, iso, herm


def nilpotent(m):
    return is_zero_matrix(m ** m.rows)


def analyse(label, symbol, zs, support, gens, sigma, results):
    dim = len(gens[0])
    cart = {h: sum((a * sp.Matrix(g) for a, g in zip(h, gens)), sp.zeros(dim, 1)) for h in support}
    L = max((sp.sqrt((v.T * v)[0]) for v in cart.values()), key=lambda x: float(x))
    L = sp.radsimp(sp.simplify(L))
    keys = sorted(support)
    pts = [(cart[h] / L).applyfunc(sp.radsimp) for h in keys]
    facets, verts = hull(pts)
    r_in = min(facets.values(), key=lambda x: float(x))
    at0, A, tilt, c2, iso, herm = node_speeds(symbol, zs, gens, L, sigma, dim)
    record(f"{label}: U(0) = I (node at k = 0, all quasienergies 0)", is_zero_matrix(at0 - sp.eye(symbol.rows)))
    record(f"{label}: linear generators i dU/dk_a(0) are Hermitian", herm)
    record(f"{label}: no tilt at the node (trace parts vanish)", all(t == 0 for t in tilt))
    record(f"{label}: isotropic untilted cone, {{T_a, T_b}} = 2 c0^2 delta_ab", iso)
    c0 = sp.radsimp(sp.sqrt(c2))
    survive = {keys[i]: not nilpotent(support[keys[i]]) for i in verts}
    all_survive = all(survive.values())
    record(f"{label}: every vertex coefficient is non-nilpotent (C_infty^strict = C_micro)",
           all_survive, f"{sum(survive.values())}/{len(survive)} vertices survive")
    c_strict = r_in if all_survive else None
    ratio = sp.radsimp(sp.simplify(c0 / r_in))
    print(f"  {label}: L = {L}; |vertices| = {len(verts)}; |facets| = {len(facets)}; "
          f"r_in = {r_in}; V_causal^max = 1; c0 = {c0}; c0/r_in = {ratio}; "
          f"c_strict^iso = {c_strict if c_strict is not None else 'OPEN'}; "
          f"S_min = c0/V_causal^max = {c0}; S_max = c0/r_in = {ratio}; "
          f"c0 in source units (per update, |h| as in source) = {sp.radsimp(c0 * L)}")
    exp_r, exp_c, exp_v = EXPECTED_MASSLESS[label]
    record(f"{label}: tabulated r_in = {exp_r}, c_0 = {exp_c}, |vertices| = {exp_v}",
           sp.simplify(r_in - exp_r) == 0 and sp.simplify(c0 - exp_c) == 0 and len(verts) == exp_v)
    results[label] = dict(s=symbol.rows, d=dim, r_in=r_in, c0=c0, ratio=ratio,
                          c_strict=c_strict, iso=iso, nverts=len(verts))
    return results[label]

# ================================================================ Part 3
# Massive rows. With U(0) = exp(-i theta Q), Q^2 = I, Duhamel's formula gives
# i dU U(0)^dag = X_par + (sin theta/theta) X_perp exp(i theta Q), where
# X_par / X_perp are the parts of dH commuting / anticommuting with Q
# (corrected 2026-11-01 after both reviews; the earlier comment omitted the
# unitary factor). The code forms B = Y_par + (theta/sin theta) Y_perp from
# Y = i dU U(0)^dag, i.e. B = X_par + X_perp exp(i theta Q). When X_par = 0
# (asserted), B is Hermitian, anticommutes with Q, and {B_a, B_b} =
# {X_a, X_b}: its Gram matrix is that of dH, which defines c_H (linear
# spatial coefficient of H = i log U at fixed mass, as in the Weyl/Dirac
# gate). B itself is not dH. With
# G = (U + U^dag)/2 scalar at k = 0 and zero linear term, cos omega_j(k) are
# cos theta + eig(G_2(k)) + O(k^3), so omega_j^2 = theta^2 + (theta/sin theta)
# q_j |k|^2 with G_2 = -(q/2)|k|^2 (c_omega2^2 = theta q / sin theta).

THETA = sp.Symbol("theta", positive=True)


def massive(label, symbol, zs, gens, scale, dim, Q, c0, theta_note):
    """Certify the exact first-order Hamiltonian Gram matrix g_ab
    ({B_a, B_b} = 2 g_ab I, c_H(n)^2 = n.g.n) and the exact decomposition
    Hess_k cos(omega)(0) = -c_0^2 cos(theta) I + sin(theta) K. Isotropy is
    reported, not assumed."""
    k = sp.symbols(f"k1:{dim + 1}", real=True)
    subs = {z: sp.exp(-sp.I * sum((k[a] * g[a] for a in range(dim)), 0) / scale)
            for z, g in zip(zs, gens)}
    s_dim = symbol.rows
    ident = sp.eye(s_dim)
    U = symbol.subs(subs)
    zero = {ka: 0 for ka in k}
    simp = lambda e: sp.simplify(sp.expand_complex(sp.expand(e)))
    U0 = U.subs(zero).applyfunc(simp)
    expected = sp.cos(THETA) * ident - sp.I * sp.sin(THETA) * Q
    record(f"{label} massive: U(0) = exp(-i theta Q), Q^2 = I ({theta_note})",
           is_zero_matrix(U0 - expected) and is_zero_matrix(Q * Q - ident))
    B = []
    par_zero = True
    for a in range(dim):
        Y = ((sp.I * U.diff(k[a])).subs(zero) * expected.H).applyfunc(simp)
        Xpar = (Y + Q * Y * Q) / 2
        Xperp = (Y - Q * Y * Q) / 2
        par_zero = par_zero and is_zero_matrix(Xpar)
        B.append((Xpar + THETA / sp.sin(THETA) * Xperp).applyfunc(sp.simplify))
    record(f"{label} massive: linear dH anticommutes with the mass term (X_par = 0)", par_zero)
    gram = sp.zeros(dim, dim)
    scalar = True
    for a in range(dim):
        for b in range(dim):
            anti = ((B[a] * B[b] + B[b] * B[a]) / 2).applyfunc(sp.simplify)
            gram[a, b] = sp.simplify(anti[0, 0])
            scalar = scalar and is_zero_matrix(anti - gram[a, b] * ident)
    record(f"{label} massive: {{B_a, B_b}} = 2 g_ab I (scalar Gram matrix)", scalar)
    g_rel = (gram / (c0 ** 2 * (THETA / sp.sin(THETA)) ** 2)).applyfunc(sp.simplify)
    iso_H = is_zero_matrix(g_rel - g_rel[0, 0] * sp.eye(dim))  # Gram proportional to I
    Ud = U.H.applyfunc(lambda e: e.subs({sp.conjugate(THETA): THETA}))
    G = (U + Ud) / 2
    record(f"{label} massive: grad cos(omega)(0) = 0 (no linear dispersion term)",
           all(is_zero_matrix(G.diff(ka).subs(zero).applyfunc(simp)) for ka in k))
    K = {}
    decomposed = True
    for a in range(dim):
        for b in range(dim):
            hab = G.diff(k[a]).diff(k[b]).subs(zero).applyfunc(simp)
            rest = (hab + (c0 ** 2 * sp.cos(THETA) if a == b else 0) * ident).applyfunc(
                lambda e: sp.simplify(e / sp.sin(THETA)))
            decomposed = decomposed and all(not e.has(THETA) for e in rest)
            K[(a, b)] = rest
    record(f"{label} massive: Hess cos(omega)(0) = -c_0^2 cos(theta) I + sin(theta) K, "
           f"K independent of theta", decomposed)
    iso_W = all(is_zero_matrix(m) for m in K.values())
    print(f"  {label}: c_H Gram / (c_0 theta/sin theta)^2 = {g_rel.tolist()} -> "
          f"{('ISOTROPIC c_H, c_H^2/c_0^2 = ' + str(sp.simplify(g_rel[0, 0] * (THETA / sp.sin(THETA)) ** 2))) if iso_H else 'ANISOTROPIC c_H(n) at finite mass'}")
    Kshow = {ab: (m[0, 0] if is_zero_matrix(m - m[0, 0] * ident) else m.tolist()) for ab, m in K.items()}
    print(f"  {label}: curvature correction K_ab = {Kshow} -> "
          f"{'ISOTROPIC c_omega2 = c_0 sqrt(theta cot theta)' if iso_W else 'NO isotropic c_omega2 at finite mass (anisotropic and/or band-split, O(sin theta))'}")
    return dict(iso_H=iso_H, iso_W=iso_W, gram=g_rel, K=K)

def residual(expression):

def check_zero(label, expression):

def laurent_adjoint(matrix, variables):
    return matrix.H.subs({variable: 1 / variable for variable in variables}, simultaneous=True)


def published_symbol(dimension, chirality, variables):
    cosines = [(variable + 1 / variable) / 2 for variable in variables]
    sines = [(variable - 1 / variable) / (2 * sp.I) for variable in variables]
    if dimension == 1:
        scalar = cosines[0]
        vector = (0, 0, sines[0])
    elif dimension == 2:
        cos_x, cos_y = cosines
        sin_x, sin_y = sines
        scalar = cos_x * cos_y
        vector = (sin_x * cos_y, cos_x * sin_y, sin_x * sin_y)
    else:
        cos_x, cos_y, cos_z = cosines
        sin_x, sin_y, sin_z = sines
        scalar = cos_x * cos_y * cos_z + chirality * sin_x * sin_y * sin_z
        vector = (
            sin_x * cos_y * cos_z - chirality * cos_x * sin_y * sin_z,
            -chirality * cos_x * sin_y * cos_z - sin_x * cos_y * sin_z,
            cos_x * cos_y * sin_z - chirality * sin_x * sin_y * cos_z,
        )
    matrix = scalar * IDENTITY
    for component, pauli in zip(vector, PAULI):
        matrix -= sp.I * component * pauli
    return matrix.applyfunc(sp.expand), sp.expand(scalar)


def rotation_product(dimension, chirality, variables):
    axes = (2,) if dimension == 1 else tuple(range(dimension))
    signs = (1, -chirality, 1) if dimension == 3 else (1,) * dimension
    matrix = IDENTITY
    for variable, axis, sign in zip(variables, axes, signs):
        positive = (IDENTITY - sign * PAULI[axis]) / 2
        negative = (IDENTITY + sign * PAULI[axis]) / 2
        matrix = matrix * (variable * positive + negative / variable)
    return matrix.applyfunc(sp.expand)


def fourier_support(matrix, variables):
    shift = sp.prod(variables)
    coefficients = {}
    for row in range(matrix.rows):
        for column in range(matrix.cols):
            polynomial = sp.Poly(sp.expand(shift * matrix[row, column]), *variables)
            for powers, coefficient in polynomial.terms():
                displacement = tuple(1 - power for power in powers)
                coefficients.setdefault(displacement, sp.zeros(matrix.rows, matrix.cols))
                coefficients[displacement][row, column] += coefficient
    return {
        displacement: coefficient
        for displacement, coefficient in coefficients.items()
        if any(sp.expand(entry) != 0 for entry in coefficient)
    }


def hull_geometry(support, dimension):
    vertices = [sp.Matrix(displacement) / sp.sqrt(dimension) for displacement in support]
    facets = set()
    for selected in itertools.combinations(vertices, dimension):
        differences = sp.Matrix(dimension - 1, dimension, lambda row, column:
                                selected[row + 1][column] - selected[0][column])
        normals = differences.nullspace()
        if len(normals) != 1:
            continue
        normal = normals[0]
        offset = normal.dot(selected[0])
        if offset == 0:
            continue
        normalized = normal / offset
        distances = [sp.simplify(normalized.dot(vertex) - 1) for vertex in vertices]
        if all(distance <= 0 for distance in distances):
            facets.add(tuple(normalized))
    if not facets:
        raise ValueError("Hull has no certified facets")
    inradius = min(sp.simplify(1 / sp.sqrt(sum(value**2 for value in facet)))
                   for facet in facets)
    maximum = max(sp.sqrt(vertex.dot(vertex)) for vertex in vertices)
    return inradius, maximum, len(facets)


def spatial_derivative(expression, variable, dimension):

                              5.4. 3D cones and rate functions
   Some quantum walks on the three-dimensional cubic lattice were proposed by Bialinycki-Birula [6]
as discrete approximations to the Weyl, Dirac, and Maxwell equations. The interest in that paper
is mainly in the continuum limit, and the possible variations of nearest neighbour walks automata
for which this works. But one can take the discrete model as a system in its own right and analyze
its propagation properties. Let us consider the simplest of these, the “Weyl equation” model in [6].
We then have K = C2 , and
                                 W (p1 , p2 , p3 ) = eip1 σ1 eip2 σ2 eip3 σ3 .                 (5.12)

This gives
                                                                                    
                ω± (p) = ±ω(p) = ± arccos cos p1 cos p2 cos p3 − sin p1 sin p2 sin p3 .        (5.13)

A characteristic feature here are conical singularities at p = 0 and eight inequivalent points in the
Brillouin zone, where p1 , p2 , p3 ∈ πZ, meaning that all cos pi = ±1 and each matrix factor in (5.12)
is ±1. Expanding the cosine of (5.13) to second order in a small deviation p from such a conical
point p∨ , we get, for any combination of the signs cos(p∨ k ),

                                ω(p∨ + p)2 = (p21 + p21 + p21 ) + O(p3 )                       (5.14)

This is in keeping with the remarks in Section 2.2 on regular vs. singular points in a dispersion
relation. Along a straight line through p∨ we can use one-parameter perturbation theory, in which
we can always choose analytic branches, corresponding to the straight lines in the boundary of the
cone, but as a function of three parameters ω(p + p∨ ) ≈ ω(p∨ ) ± |p| is not analytic. In the limit
