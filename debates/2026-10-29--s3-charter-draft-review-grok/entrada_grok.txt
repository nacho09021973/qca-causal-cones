## Method

**Group.**
- `O` is the set of 24 proper signed permutation matrices. All arithmetic is with integers.
- Every subgroup of `S4 ≅ O` is generated by at most two elements, so closing all ordered pairs
  gives every subgroup. The closure of a pair is the subgroup it generates: in a finite group,
  `g^{-1} = g^{n-1}` when `g^n = 1`, so the generated monoid is a subgroup. The proof is by order
  (G-S0-02, completed after the Grok round-2 review, which supplied the arguments). By Lagrange a
  subgroup has order 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 or 24. Element orders in `S4` are 1, 2 (transpositions,
  double transpositions), 3 (3-cycles) and 4 (4-cycles).
  - **No `C2³`.** The 6 transpositions form one class, so the centralizer of `(12)` has order
    `24/6 = 4`: it is `{1, (12), (34), (12)(34)}`. An elementary abelian 2-subgroup containing a
    transposition therefore has order ≤ 4. One without transpositions lies in
    `{1} ∪ {double transpositions}`, which is the normal `V4`, of order 4.
  - **Orders 1–4:** cyclic or `C2×C2`, both 2-generated.
  - **Order 6:** a group of order 6 is `C6` or `S3`. There is no element of order 6, so it is
    `S3 = ⟨(12),(13)⟩`, since `(12)(13)(12) = (23)`.
  - **Order 8:** it is a 2-Sylow. The 2-Sylows are conjugate, and one of them is the dihedral
    `⟨r, s⟩` (`r⁴ = s² = 1`, `srs = r⁻¹`), which is 2-generated. Equivalently: of the five groups
    of order 8, only `C2³` is not 2-generated, and it is excluded above.
  - **Order 12:** index 2, hence normal. The only index-2 subgroup is the kernel of the sign,
    `A4`. Conjugating `(124)` by `(123)` gives `(234)`; further conjugations give all eight
    3-cycles, which generate `A4`. So `A4 = ⟨(123),(124)⟩`.
  - **Order 24:** with `b = (1234)` and `a = (12)`, `bab⁻¹ = (23)` and `b(23)b⁻¹ = (34)`. The
    transpositions `(12), (23), (34)` generate `S4`, so `S4 = ⟨(12),(1234)⟩`.

  The certificate finds 30 subgroups. That number is a cross-check, not the proof.

**Family P.**
- All 30 subgroups are crossed with all 16 choices of `S_+`, keeping the pairs where `G`
  stabilizes `S_+` and is transitive on it.
- Classes are taken up to `O`-conjugation of the pair.
- The 16 choices of `S_+` are 2 tetrahedra, 6 faces and 8 "vertex plus three neighbours" sets.

**Family F.** The subgroups transitive on all eight vertices, up to conjugation.

**Representations.**
- A 2-dimensional projective unitary `R`, up to projective equivalence `R' = c W R W^†`, is a
  homomorphism `ρ: G → PU(2) ≅ SO(3)` up to `SO(3)` conjugation. Covariance
  `T_{gh} = R_g T_h R_g^†` depends only on `ρ`.
- Every image type that occurs (1, `C2`, `C3`, `C4`, `V4`, `D3`, `D4`, `A4`, `S4`) is
  `SO(3)`-conjugate to a subgroup of `O`, so enumerating homomorphisms into `O` is exhaustive.
  For these five domains the counts do not need the general classification of finite subgroups
  of `SO(3)`:
  - the images are quotients of the domain;
  - the trace of a rotation is fixed by its order: 3, −1, 0, 1 for orders 1, 2, 3, 4.

  This enumerates the possible characters directly; the Grok review carries it out group by
  group.
- Two classes are identified iff their 3-dimensional characters agree. Equal real characters
  give orthogonal equivalence, and replacing `M` by `−M` makes it `SO(3)`.
- `R` is reducible iff the trivial multiplicity of `ρ` is at least 1, meaning a fixed axis.
- The projective kernel of `R` is `ker ρ`.

**Kernel lemma (A1, A6, A7).**
- If `R` is scalar on `K = ker ρ`, then `T` is constant on `K`-orbits.
- Take a shift `ℓ != 0` whose pairs `(h, h+ℓ)` all lie inside `K`-orbits. Its unitarity identity
  `Σ T_h^† T_{h+ℓ} = 0` becomes a sum of positive semidefinite terms `T_h^† T_h`.
- So every `T_h` that appears vanishes, which contradicts `T_h != 0`.
- The certificate prints the shift and the pairs for every emptied class.

- Each shift `2h` has the single pair `(−h, h)`. With `T_{−h}^† T_h = 0` and both nonzero, both
  have rank one.
- Cayley–Hamilton, `T^2 = tr(T) T − det(T) I`, is checked symbolically.

## 0. Correction and baseline certificate (2026-09-14)

The gate was originally written as a comparison against the inradius of the
*one-step* microscopic causal body. That object is only an upper bound on the
speed actually reachable by strict propagation after repeated updates, and
the inradius notation is not the right general comparison for a long-run
support body. The correct comparison target is the **strict asymptotic
isotropic speed** `c_strict^iso = min_{n̂} h_infty(n̂)`, defined in
[`CAUSAL_BODY_GEOMETRY.md`](CAUSAL_BODY_GEOMETRY.md) Sec. 6 from
`S_n = {x : the Fourier coefficient of U^n at x is nonzero}`.

For every direction `q`, define

\[
H_n(q)=\max_{x\in S_n}q\cdot x.
\]

The convolution formula for the Fourier coefficients gives

\[
S_{n+m}\subseteq S_n+S_m
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
H_{n+m}(q)\le H_n(q)+H_m(q).
\]

Thus `(H_n(q))_n` is subadditive, and Fekete's lemma gives

\[
h_\infty(q)
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{H_n(q)}n
=\inf_n\frac{H_n(q)}n.
\]

In particular, grouping `m` updates into one coarse-grained temporal step
does not change the normalized asymptotic support function:

\[
\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{H_{mr}(q)}{mr}=h_\infty(q).
\]

This proves the coarse-graining invariance of `c_strict^iso` and is the
mathematical reason for using the `B_\infty^*` observable. Since
`S_n/n\subseteq C_{\rm micro}`, the general comparison is instead

\[
h_\infty(\hat q)\le h_{C_{\rm micro}}(\hat q),
\qquad
\rho_{\rm micro}:=
\min_{\|\hat q\|=1}h_{C_{\rm micro}}(\hat q),
\qquad
\boxed{
c_{\rm strict}^{\rm iso}
\le
\rho_{\rm micro}
}.
\]

Only when the relevant `C_micro` is centered does this last number coincide
with its usual inradius. For the centered cubic baseline used below it is

These dimensions are standard and pass the sum-of-squares check (12+12 = 24, 24+24 = 48).

| Equivalences | Constant basis changes commuting with `R` up to the declared projective class, global phases, node relabelings within an orbit |

Consequences of the declaration, to be derived exactly in Step 1 and not assumed. Under
`O`, which is transitive on `N_3`, the eight coefficients are fixed by one `T_{h0}`. Under
`T`, they are fixed by one coefficient on each tetrahedron, `T_{h0}` and `T_{-h0}`. In both
cases each fixed coefficient must commute with `R` restricted to the order-3 stabilizer of
its vertex.

## 3. Sequence

**Step 0 — sources and controls (no new scientific claim).**

1. Read the isotropy definitions and classification assumptions in the primary sources
   already used for the Pavia baseline. These include D'Ariano–Erba–Perinotti,
   arXiv:1708.00826 (local copy in `biblioteca/`), and the source of the "P17
   classification" cited in CURRENT_STATE Sec. 4. Record which group, which `s` and which
   representation they quantify over. Check whether the question of Sec. 1 is already
   answered there. Unread items stay `[UNVERIFIED]`.
2. Verify the irreducible dimensions of `2T` and `2O` from a primary source or from an
   exact character-table computation kept in `scripts/`.
3. **Control A:** determine exactly whether the standard Pavia `s=2` Weyl symbols are `T`-
   and `O`-covariant with a 2-dimensional spinorial irreducible representation. This is a
   consistency control of the classes, not a result.
4. **Control B (decisive first test for O4):** determine exactly whether the s=4 family
   `A_a` admits `O`-covariance for some `R` and, if so, whether that `R` can be the
   irreducible 4-dimensional one.
   - If yes for some `0 < a < 1`, with all O4 admission checks passing and the existing
     `c_strict^iso` certificate applying unchanged, the outcome is
     `COUNTEREXAMPLE_VERIFIED` for class O4 and the gate stops for O4.
   - If `A_a` is not `O`-covariant with that `R`, record `S4_FAMILY_OUTSIDE_O4` and
     continue. This is not evidence for rigidity.
   - The family has `s = 4` and is therefore outside T3 and O3 by definition.

**Step 1 — exact class derivation.** For each class, derive the constraints on `T_{h0}`
from stabilizer covariance and the unitarity identities of the predecessor's Sec. 3, before
choosing any ansatz. Record the dimension and connected components of the solution set if
they can be determined exactly. If the class is empty, the outcome is `EMPTY_CLASS` for
that class.

**Step 2 — massless sectors.** Within the derived class, find the admissible gapless
sectors and their linear slopes `c_0`, exactly.

**Step 3 — strict body and comparison.** For each admissible candidate, certify
`c_strict^iso` exactly. Use the vertex lemma when every vertex coefficient is
non-nilpotent; otherwise compute it, because vertex nilpotency alone decides nothing
(predecessor erratum). Then compare it with `c_0`.

## 4. Admission, stop rules and budget

- Admission checks are those of the predecessor's Sec. 3, with `O` and the declared
  irreducible `R`. The first failed check makes a candidate `INADMISSIBLE_CANDIDATE`, and its
  certification stops there.
- Exact algebra only. A finite scan or a numerical search may suggest candidates but
  certifies nothing. Every retained computation is a versioned script in `scripts/`, run
  through `run_all_verifiers.py` before commit.
- Stop for a class at its first terminal outcome (Sec. 5).
- Budget: Steps 0–1 for the three classes are one authorization. Steps 2–3 need a separate
  user authorization after Step 1 is reported, because their cost depends on the dimension
  of the class found there.
- If Step 1 shows the constraint system is not tractable exactly within that
  authorization, stop with `OPEN_AFTER_CLASS_DERIVATION` and report what was derived.

## 5. Outcomes and claim ceilings (per class)

| Outcome | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `COUNTEREXAMPLE_VERIFIED` | An admissible symbol in the class with `0 < c_0 < c_strict^iso`, exactly certified. Irreducibility under `O` does not force matching for that `s`. |
| `RIGIDITY_PROVED_FOR_FROZEN_CLASS` | `c_0 = c_strict^iso` proved for every admissible member, with every restriction retained. Says nothing about other `s`, other groups or reducible `R`. |
| `EMPTY_CLASS` | No symbol satisfies the class constraints. The hypothesis is vacuous for that `s`, which is a first-class negative result. |
| `INADMISSIBLE_SATURATION` | As in the predecessor: (i) holds but `c_strict^iso < rho_micro`. Not a rigidity counterexample. |
| `S4_FAMILY_OUTSIDE_O4` | Control B only: the known s=4 family is not `O`-covariant with the irreducible 4-dimensional `R`. It decides nothing by itself. |
| `OPEN_AFTER_CLASS_DERIVATION` / `INCONCLUSIVE` | No certified counterexample, no exhaustive proof. |

Ceilings retained: no result here establishes A (species universality), matching under
`D2`, or any statement about nature. A rigidity result would mean that, under the declared
group and irreducible `R` at that `s`, `c_0` is fixed by the strict causal geometry. It would not
derive the numerical value of `c`, since the absolute scale is not derivable
(`NO_ABSOLUTE_SCALE_DERIVABLE`, Sec. 8.21). Novelty requires a separate source comparison.
No causal sets, random lattices, interactions, anisotropic detours or new substrate classes
are authorized by this charter.
