## 1. Statement under audit

**The predicate is a geometric classification suggested by the existing

This contract states only (PH) on the promoted rows. No general statement

**Zonotope reading (PS′) [UNVERIFIED], product class only, not equivalent
to (PH).** For a walk that is a product of `n` coin–partial-shift factors,
each partial shift moving by `±a_i`, the composite step set is
`F = {sum_i ε_i a_i}` and `C_micro = conv F` is the zonotope
`sum_i [-a_i, a_i]` (convex hull of a Minkowski sum is the Minkowski sum
of the hulls). It is a parallelepiped exactly when the reduced generators
(`a_i` up to sign and positive scale, parallel ones merged) are `d`
linearly independent vectors. (PS′) reads: in the product class, the
ceiling is attained iff the generators reduce to `d` independent
directions. **(PS′) is not equivalent to (PH):** `d` independent
generators need not be orthogonal or of equal length, so a parallelepiped
need not be a hypercube (e.g. a 60° rhombus in `d = 2`). No equivalence is
claimed until proved. Examples in the promoted rows: square (two
generators), BCC/cube (three orthogonal generators), `W_hex` (three
generators at 120°, `d = 2`: the hexagon). The zonotope facts it would use
(e.g. the space-tiling criterion of Shephard 1974 and McMullen 1975) are
cited by Moriyama–Otozawa (arXiv:2411.09141v1) and have not been read
here. Phase 0 records the reduced generators of each product-class row

## 1. Conjetura — REPAIR_REQUIRED

La bicondicional está parseada: (PS⇐) es existencial y (PS⇒) es universal, y el hexágono (6 vértices) no es un paralelotopo (en `d = 2`, 4 vértices). Entre las formas ya tabuladas, eso corta mejor que «zonotopo»: segmento, cuadrado, cubo y hexágono son zonotopos, y el hexágono no satura en los walks declarados. Tampoco es «simetría suficiente para un cono isótropo». L6a, L6c, L6d y L6f tienen cono isótropo sin inclinación y `c_0 < r_in`; el hexágono tiene más simetría que el cuadrado y no alcanza el techo.

El fallo es la frase de la Sec. 1, leída con la Sec. 2.3: el techo «se alcanza […] si y solo si su `C_micro` es un paralelotopo», y paralelotopo es «la suma de Minkowski de `d` segmentos linealmente independientes (un paralelogramo en `d = 2`)». Eso incluye un rombo de 60° y un paralelepípedo que no es cubo. `c_0` solo existe si hay nodo isótropo sin inclinación (`{T_a, T_b} = 2 c_0^2 δ_ab`). El grupo de simetría de un rombo de 60° deja dos ejes invariantes: no fuerza una única forma cuadrática. (PS⇐) afirma entonces un `c_0` que puede no estar definido. La cláusula `OUTSIDE_DEFINITIONS` de la Sec. 3.4 aparca una fila ya declarada; no arregla el enunciado general que la fase 1 heredaría («PS⇐ para paralelotopos generales»).

Las filas que sí saturan son hipercubos, salvo isometría: segmento, cuadrado (L2 y L6e) y cubo (BCC: la envolvente de `{±1}^3`; L4: `[-1,1]^3`). No hay en los adjuntos ningún paralelotopo que no sea hipercubo, así que nada distingue las dos nociones y nada contradice la letra de (PS). L3s4 no la contradice: es `s = 4` y, además, en ese sustrato L3 y L5a sí saturan.

## 2. Cuantificadores y dominio — REPAIR_REQUIRED

El existencial y el universal están bien separados, y un walk que no satura no refuta (PS⇐) si otro del mismo sustrato satura. «Sustrato» queda definido por la cita al contrato de retículos: grupo de traslaciones generado por el paso compuesto, más ese paso. L6a y L6d son el mismo sustrato; L6c es otro, porque el grupo es más fino, aunque la forma de `C_micro` coincida. Eso es correcto.

Hay dos errores de dominio. Primero, la Sec. 2.2: «L6b with `s = 6`» queda fuera. El contrato de retículos y el informe de ejecución ponen L6b en `s = 2` (en el informe, además, `OPEN`, no codificado). La síntesis, posterior, dice `s = 6` y da `c_0 = 1/3`. La fase 0 no puede excluir la fila por `s ≠ 2` mientras esas fuentes no digan lo mismo. Segundo, la Sec. 1 restringe (PS) a «the declared classes», y la Sec. 6 reutiliza el mismo nombre para «PS⇐ for general parallelotopes». Son dos enunciados. Un `NOT_REFUTED` sobre la lista finita no es la supervivencia del enunciado general.

## 3. Normalización — REPAIR_REQUIRED

`C_micro = conv(N)`, `r_in = min h_C`, cono isótropo sin inclinación y paso más largo 1 ponen en la misma escala a las filas cuyo par `(c_0, r_in)` se copie de una sola fuente ya normalizada. La igualdad `c_0 = r_in` no cambia si se reescalan los dos a la vez.

El agujero es L4. En CS Sec. 8, `C_micro = [-1,1]^3`, `r_in = 1` y `c_IR = 1`. El informe de retículos (Sec. 4) y la síntesis reescalan a paso más largo 1: `r_in = c_0 = 1/√3`. La Sec. 2.3 dice que un valor «taken in other units, is not a comparison», pero no dice que L4 tiene los dos sistemas promocionados. El método pide anotar los valores exactos: cruzar `c_0` de una fuente con `r_in` de la otra fabrica `c_0 ≠ r_in`.

El cero en `N` no rompe la comparación. En L6a, L6c y L6f está en el interior: no cambia la envolvente, los vértices, `r_in` ni el paso más largo. Aclaración, no error.

   causal body bounds the relativistic speed.
3. **Whether the ceiling is reached depends on the substrate and the
   walk.** On the line, square, standard BCC and cube substrates, some
   declared walks reach it (`c_0 = r_in`); on the same BCC step set L3s4

4. **No absolute value.** The repo derives no value of `c` in physical
   units: in the frozen flat class the absolute scale is
   `NO_ABSOLUTE_SCALE_DERIVABLE`. Every number here is in lattice units.

This is a statement about declared rows and classes, not a QCA law.

## 1. L0 — the ceiling `c_0 <= r_in` on `W_hex`

Let `U` be in `W_hex`. Multiplying by the scalar `e^{i phi}` does not change
the support, so take `U(0) = I`. Let `n_f` be the unit outward normal of a
facet of the regular hexagon `C = conv F`, with edge vector `d in Lambda`
(edges join lattice vertices). For a lattice vector `h = a g_1 + b g_2` and
`d = p g_1 + q g_2`,

`h . Rot90(d) = (sqrt3/2)(b p - a q)`   (verifier, check L0),

so `h . n_f = m_h delta` with `m_h in Z` and `delta = sqrt3/(2|d|)`. The
extreme values of `h . n_f` over `F` are `±h_C(n_f)` (central symmetry of
the hexagon) and are attained at vertices, which lie in `F`; hence
`h_C(n_f) = N delta` for an integer `N` and every `m_h` satisfies
`|m_h| <= N`.

For unit vectors `x, y in C^2` set `p(s) = <y, U(s n_f / delta) x>`. Then
`p(s) = sum_{|m|<=N} c_m e^{-i m s}` is a trigonometric polynomial of degree
`N` with `|p| <= 1` (U unitary). Bernstein's inequality (Lemma B below,
proved here) gives `|p'(0)| <= N`, i.e. `|<y, dU/dt(0) x>| <= N delta =
h_C(n_f)` along `n_f`. Taking the supremum over `x, y`,
`||A(n_f)|| <= h_C(n_f) = r_in`, where `A(n) = i dU(t n)/dt (0)`. `A(n_f)` is
Hermitian: differentiating `U U^dag = I` at `t = 0`, where `U = I`, shows
`dU/dt(0)` is anti-Hermitian. With `s = 2` and a traceless isotropic cone,
`A(n_f)^2 = c_0^2 I`, so the Hermitian `A(n_f)` has spectrum `{±c_0}` and
`||A(n_f)|| = c_0` (without Hermiticity, `M^2 = c_0^2 I` would not fix the
norm). Therefore **`c_0 <= r_in` for every walk in `W_hex`**. Only the
facet normal is needed, since `r_in = min h_C` is attained there.

**Lemma B (Bernstein, proved in-repo; repair R1 of the 2026-11-02 Grok
review).** If `p(s) = sum_{|m|<=N} c_m e^{-ims}` and `|p| <= 1` on `R`, then
`|p'(s_0)| <= N` for every `s_0`.

*Reduction to real polynomials.* Fix `s_0` and choose `alpha` with
`e^{i alpha} p'(s_0) = |p'(s_0)|`. `R(s) = Re(e^{i alpha} p(s))` is a real
trigonometric polynomial of degree `<= N`, `|R| <= 1`, and
`R'(s_0) = Re(e^{i alpha} p'(s_0)) = |p'(s_0)|`. So it suffices to show
`R'(s_0) <= N` for real `R`.

*Real case.* Suppose `R'(s_0) > N`. Choose `0 < eps < 1` so small that
`R_eps = (1 - eps) R` still has `R_eps'(s_0) > N`; then `|R_eps| < 1`
everywhere. Pick `beta in (-pi, 0)` with `cos beta = R_eps(s_0)` (possible
since `|R_eps(s_0)| < 1`) and set `C(s) = cos(N(s - s_0) + beta)`. Then
`C(s_0) = R_eps(s_0)` and `C'(s_0) = -N sin beta in (0, N]`, so
`D = C - R_eps` has `D(s_0) = 0`, `D'(s_0) < 0`; in particular `D` is not
identically zero. On one period `C` reaches `±1` alternately at `2N`
points `t_1 < ... < t_2N`, and there `|R_eps| < 1` gives
`sign D(t_j) = sign C(t_j)`, alternating. Hence `D` has a zero in each of
the `2N` open arcs between consecutive `t_j`. The point `s_0` is not a
`t_j` (`D(s_0) = 0 != D(t_j)`), so it lies in one arc `(t_j, t_{j+1})`, on
which `C` is monotone; `C'(s_0) > 0` makes it increasing, so
`C(t_j) = -1`, `C(t_{j+1}) = +1`, i.e. `D(t_j) < 0 < D(t_{j+1})`. Since `D`
falls through zero at `s_0` (`D'(s_0) < 0`), `D > 0` just left of `s_0` and
`D < 0` just right of it: there is a further zero in `(t_j, s_0)` and one in
`(s_0, t_{j+1})`. That arc holds at least three zeros, so `D` has at least
`2N + 2` zeros per period. But `e^{iNs} D(s)` is an algebraic polynomial of
degree `<= 2N` in `z = e^{is}`, so a nonzero `D` has at most `2N` zeros per
period. Contradiction; hence `R'(s_0) <= N`. Applying this to `R(-s)` gives
`R'(s_0) >= -N`. `square`

This is the classical argument (prior art; no novelty claimed); it is
written out so that L0 closes in-repo as the contract (Sec. 2) requires.

## 4. Derivation of the entries

**`F`, `C_micro`.** In an up triangle the anticlockwise moves between side
midpoints point at 60°, 180°, 300°; in a down triangle at 240°, 0°, 120°.
The move out of an edge on the grey side is minus the move on the white
side. So `F` is six vectors of length 1/2 (side units) and `C_micro` the
regular hexagon, `r_in = sqrt3/2` normalised.

**Node.** `U(0) = P ⊗ W` with `P` the cyclic permutation of the three
edge orientations, so `U(0)^3 = I`. Its spectrum is `{1, e^{±2πi/3}}`, each
with multiplicity 2; the eigenvalue-1 pair is the declared node.

**`c_0`, two routes.** (A) `V = U^3` is block diagonal in edge orientation,
hence a walk on the white-triangle Bravais lattice with `V(0) = I`; the
parent's `node_speeds` gives a Hermitian, untilted, isotropic cone with
`c_0(V) = 1/2` side per three updates. The node branch of `U` has
quasienergy `omega = Omega/3`, so per update `c_0 = 1/6` side, `1/3`
normalised. (B) First-order degenerate perturbation of `U` itself on the
eigenvalue-1 space: generator `Q^dagger (i U(0)^dagger dU/dk) Q`,
untilted, isotropic, same `c_0`. Both routes agree exactly.

**`c_strict^iso`.** Every one-step vertex coefficient is nilpotent: the
edge orientation advances at every update, so the same move cannot be
repeated. With `h_infty(n̂) = limsup_n (1/n) max_{x in S_n} n̂·x`
(`theory/B_STAR_HYPOTHESIS.md`, from `CAUSAL_BODY_GEOMETRY.md` Sec. 6):
`supp U^3 = {0, ±2w_o}`, a hexagon of radius 1 side, and all six vertex
coefficients of `U^3` are non-nilpotent. For `V = U^3`, a translation-
invariant walk, the coefficient of `V^m` at `m v` for a vertex `v` is
`M_v^m`, nonzero; and `supp U^n` lies within a bounded distance of
`supp U^{3⌊n/3⌋}`. Hence `h_infty = h_{conv supp U^3} / 3`, inradius
`sqrt3/6` side per update, `1/sqrt3` normalised. The asymptotic body is
the hexagon `(1/3) sum_o [-w_o, w_o]`, with the same orientation as
`C_micro` (vertices at multiples of 60°) and scaled by 2/3.

## 5. Cross-checks against the source (contract Sec. 4)

- **Coefficient.** The source states `i d_t psi = (sqrt3/6)(p·sigma) psi`
  per update `epsilon`, with side-midpoint distance `sqrt3/2`, i.e. side
  `sqrt3`. The computed `c_0` in those units is `sqrt3/6`. Agrees.
- **Equivalence with L6a.** `supp U^3` equals L6a's `F` in the same
  integer coordinates (generators halved). Each orientation block of `U^3`
  has the characteristic polynomial of the L6a symbol **up to an isometry
  of the hexagon**, not in the same orientation: the first run of the
  verifier tested the identity isometry only and that check failed (all
  three blocks). Re-derived: L6b's three moves from edge `h` are at 60°,
  180°, 300°, L6a's partial shifts at 0°, 120°, 240°. The repaired check
  tries all 12 isometries; the matching ones are the rotations by 60°,
  180°, 300° and three reflections, for every block. So three L6b updates
  realise the L6a dispersion at half the length scale, rotated 60°.
